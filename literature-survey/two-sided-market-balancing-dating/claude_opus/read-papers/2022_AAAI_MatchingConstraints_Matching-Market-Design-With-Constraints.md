## Summary
This is a theory survey of two-sided matching market design (the Gale-Shapley tradition: students/hospitals, workers/firms) under real-world feasibility constraints — regional caps, diversity quotas, multi-dimensional capacities, matroidal constraints, and "heredity" constraints — that go beyond the classical model's simple per-institution upper quota. The paper unifies these extensions through a **Generalized Deferred Acceptance (GDA)** algorithm operating over contracts and choice functions, states the structural properties (substitutability, irrelevance of rejected contracts, law of aggregate demand) under which GDA is provably stable, agent-optimal, and strategyproof, and surveys constraint-specific algorithms (Adaptive Deferred Acceptance, Cutoff Stable Algorithm) and their existence/complexity results. It is purely theoretical — no experiments, no platform data, no numeric results.

## Method
Extends the basic Gale-Shapley two-sided matching model (agents N, institutions H, ordinal preferences/priorities) to a **contracts** formulation: a contract x = (i, h, c) ties agent i to institution h under contractual term c; institutions have choice functions Ch_h(X) selecting a subset of offered contracts X. The central algorithm, **Generalized Deferred Acceptance (Algorithm 1)**: agents propose their most-preferred remaining contracts; each institution's choice function selects which to (tentatively) keep; rejected contracts accumulate; repeat until no further contracts are rejected. Key structural properties of choice functions: **Substitutability (SUB)** — rejecting a contract from a larger set doesn't cause other contracts to be rejected; **Irrelevance of Rejected Contracts (IRC)** — removing already-rejected contracts from consideration doesn't change what's chosen; **Law of Aggregate Demand (LAD)** — the number of contracts chosen is monotone in the offered set size. When SUB + LAD hold, GDA yields a stable, agent-optimal, strategyproof matching. Constraint-specific extensions are then layered on: **lower quotas** (minimum-fill requirements, can break stability existence — NP-hard in general per Biró et al. 2010); **regional constraints** (upper caps on aggregated sets of institutions, e.g., urban/rural hospital regions) which break the SUB property of the aggregate choice function and require a weaker "weak stability" notion (Kamada & Kojima); **diversity constraints** (type-specific lower/upper quotas within one institution, illustrated with a worked example); **multi-dimensional capacity/agent-size constraints** (e.g., refugee family resettlement, where families of different sizes consume multiple resource dimensions — rooms, school seats); **matroidal constraints** (generalizing simple upper quotas to matroid feasibility, unifying several of the above as special cases); and **heredity constraints** (a general monotonicity property), for which the paper presents two named algorithms: **Adaptive Deferred Acceptance (ADA)** (Goto et al. 2017 — repeatedly runs standard DA over a growing "master list" of agents, freezing institutions as they become infeasible) and the **Cutoff Stable Algorithm** (Aziz, Baychkov & Biró 2021 — computes per-institution admission cutoff scores that induce a "cutoff stable" matching, a notion stronger than weak stability).

## Results
No numeric results. The paper's "results" are theorems and existence/complexity statements it surveys, e.g.: GDA is stable/agent-optimal/strategyproof when choice functions satisfy SUB+LAD (Hatfield & Milgrom 2005; Fleiner 2003); with lower quotas, determining whether a stable matching exists is **NP-hard in general** (Biró et al. 2010); under regional constraints a stable matching **may not exist** (worked counterexample given); weakly stable matchings under regional constraints can be computed via a suitably constructed choice function satisfying SUB/IRC/LAD (Kamada & Kojima 2017a/2018); for heredity constraints, ADA satisfies strategyproofness, non-wastefulness, and a fairness property weaker than weak stability (Goto et al. 2017); the Cutoff Stable Algorithm produces a "cutoff stable" outcome for any heredity-satisfying feasibility constraint (Aziz, Baychkov & Biró 2021).

## Limitations
No explicit "Limitations" section (short-form AAAI survey paper), but the authors flag several open problems inline: whether weak stability and strategyproofness are simultaneously achievable for general regional constraints is stated as an open question; the paper explicitly restricts its own scope to many-to-one matching (each agent matched to at most one institution), noting many-to-many matching (e.g., paper–reviewer assignment, course allocation) is a separate, unaddressed literature.

## Heavily Cited Prior Works
- Gale & Shapley (1962), "College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage" — foundational model and Deferred Acceptance algorithm this whole paper generalizes
- Hatfield & Milgrom (2005), "Matching with Contracts" — the contracts formalism and choice-function framework GDA is built on
- Fleiner (2003), "A Fixed-Point Approach to Stable Matchings and Some Applications" — three-stability / fixed-point proof technique underlying GDA's correctness
- Kamada & Kojima (2015, 2017a, 2017b, 2018), work on "Efficient Matching under Distributional Constraints" / regional constraints — source of weak stability concept and its algorithms
- Biró, Fleiner, Irving & Manlove (2010), "The College Admissions problem with lower and common quotas" — NP-hardness result for lower quotas
- Goto, Kojima, Kurata, Tamura & Yokoo (2017), "Designing matching mechanisms under general distributional constraints" — source of the Adaptive Deferred Acceptance (ADA) algorithm
- Aziz, Baychkov & Biró (2021), "Cutoff stability under distributional constraints with an application to summer internship matching" — source of the Cutoff Stable Algorithm

## Project Relevance
Relevant to **layer 2 (capacity-aware exposure allocation)** — this is the classic operations-research toolkit for enforcing per-user capacity (upper quotas) and guaranteed minimum exposure (lower quotas), which is structurally what "cap likes to superstar users, ensure a floor for under-shown users" requires. Also touches **layer 3 (market-design levers)** since quotas and regional/diversity constraints are themselves market-design levers a platform could impose. **Disanalogy to flag:** classical matching theory here assumes fixed, known, deterministic capacities and complete ordinal preference lists resolved in one centralized pass — it has no notion of *probabilistic* reciprocal-like-back capacity inferred from behavior, no online/continuous re-ranking, and no feedback loop between exposure and future capacity (all of which the project's dating-app setting requires). Use this as formal grounding for quota mechanisms, not as a ready-made online algorithm.
