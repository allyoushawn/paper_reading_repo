# Two-Sided Time-Independent Regret for Matching Markets with Limited Interviews

- **notebook source_id:** `1d391169`
- **extraction method:** direct PDF read (NotebookLM unavailable)

## Summary
Two-sided matching platforms (labor markets, dating, sponsorships, recruiting) let participants take cheap, non-binding "interviews" (profile views, trial pitches, screening calls) to get a noisy read on match quality before committing to one real application per round — and on the firm/receiving side, preferences may themselves be uncertain and learned over time. This paper studies bandit learning under this setup, introduces "strategic deferral" (a firm can stay vacant rather than being forced to hire) as a mechanism for handling firm-side uncertainty, and proves that a *constant* number of interviews per round (as few as two) is enough to get regret that does not grow with the time horizon T — both in a centralized setting and in decentralized settings with only coarse, anonymous firm-side feedback. This is a pure theory paper: no datasets, no empirical numbers — all results are asymptotic regret bounds with matching (or near-matching) lower bounds.

## Method
Market model M(A,F): n agents and m firms (n≤m), each side has strict, initially unknown preferences with bounded [0,1] rewards. Each round t: (1) **interview stage** — each agent picks a subset of 2–k firms to interview, generating a noisy signal of match value (not a reward) for both sides; (2) **application stage** — each agent applies to exactly one interviewed firm; each firm either hires its most-preferred applicant or "defers" (stays vacant) via a **strategic rejection policy** (Algorithm 1) that abstains from hiring when its updated preference estimate suggests it may have previously mis-ranked a rejected candidate — this "deferral" primitive prevents firms from irreversibly locking in an early estimation mistake; (3) **feedback stage** — agents observe either weak *vacancy-only* feedback V(t) (which firms are currently unfilled) or richer *anonymous hiring-change* feedback V+(t) (which firms' hiring status changed since last round, identities not revealed).

Three algorithm families are given: (a) **Centralized** — a Central Interview Allocator (CIA, Algorithm 6) runs Gale–Shapley on the current estimated preference lists each round plus a round-robin exploration firm, achieving time-independent regret. (b) **Coordinated decentralized** under weak vacancy-only feedback V(t) — agents alternate fixed-length "updating" (jointly re-coordinate via a synchronized snapshot) and "committing" phases (Algorithm 2/7), using vacancy signals as an implicit coordination channel. (c) **Coordination-free decentralized** under richer V+(t) feedback — each agent independently maintains a candidate-firm set of firms that have never rejected it or have shown a hiring change since its last rejection (Algorithm 3, k=2 interviews, suffices in "α-reducible" markets); for markets with multiple stable matchings, a randomized variant (Algorithm 9, k=3) is needed to break cyclic blocking-pair dynamics. A key structural condition, **α-reducible markets**, defines a class of markets with a unique stable matching (built by iteratively removing "fixed pairs" — mutual top choices), less restrictive than serial dictatorship, and is the setting where the strongest (polynomial, not exponential) bounds hold.

## Results
All bounds are stated in Big-O notation over n (agents), m (firms), and Δ (minimum reward gap) — there are no numeric/empirical values, confidence intervals, or p-values, consistent with this being a regret-bound theory paper:
- **Centralized (Theorem 3.1, Algorithm 6/CIA):** agent regret is O(nm² min{Δ̄_A, Δ_F}⁻²), time-independent — i.e., regret converges to a constant rather than continuing to grow with T. This is shown to be within a factor of m of the information-theoretic lower bound Ω(nm) (also proved in the paper).
- **Coordinated decentralized, vacancy-only feedback V(t) (Theorem 4.1, Algorithm 7 + Algorithm 1):** O(n³m² Δ⁻²) regret in α-reducible (unique-stable-matching) markets; O(n⁴m² Δ⁻²) in general markets — within factors of m and nm of the centralized optimum, characterized as "the decentralization cost of GS [Gale-Shapley]."
- **Coordination-free decentralized, anonymous hiring-change feedback V+(t) (Theorem 4.2, Algorithm 3 with k=2 interviews for α-reducible markets, Algorithm 9 with k=3 for general markets):** time-independent regret of O(Σ|H_{a,f}|+1)·(Σ(m²Δ_aj⁻²+nmΔ_fj⁻²)+1)) in α-reducible markets; O(ε⁻¹n⁵m⁴Δ⁻²) in general markets, where ε=(λ(1−λ)^{n−1})^{n⁴m+nm} for a randomization parameter λ∈(0,1) used to break cyclic blocking-pair resolutions.
- **Headline claim:** two interviews per round suffice for horizon-independent regret converging to an actual stable matching (not merely a zero-regret non-stable outcome) — this resolves an open conjecture from Bhaskara et al. (2023), who showed three "hints" suffice under UCB-V in the simpler single-agent hinted-bandit setting but conjectured three were necessary; this paper shows two suffice, and extends the result to the harder two-sided setting with uncertain firms and strategic deferral.

## Limitations
- The centralized bound (O(nm²·)) is only proven within a factor of m of the Ω(nm) information-theoretic lower bound; the authors explicitly state they "conjecture O(nm²) is tight" but leave closing this gap "as an open problem."
- The coordination-free decentralized bound for general (non-α-reducible) markets carries a large constant from cyclic blocking-pair dynamics — the paper notes this is "common in [prior work, ref. 19] but unlike [ref. 21] absent from our structured-market bounds," i.e., an acknowledged weakness specific to the unstructured case.
- "Tight lower bounds here remain a compelling open problem" for the decentralized settings generally — only the centralized case has a matching-order lower bound.
- The paper's main-body analysis is restricted to α-reducible markets (unique stable matching); general markets get weaker/more complex bounds relegated substantially to the appendix.
- The authors explicitly disclaim real-world applicability of the motivating examples: "a detailed domain-specific mapping and performance analysis for any particular application would require substantial additional modeling effort and is beyond the scope of this work" (Appendix A) — i.e., no claim is made that the bounds hold with real dating/hiring/sponsorship data or behavior.
- Each firm's hiring capacity is fixed at exactly 1 per round in the model; no treatment of variable/multi-slot capacity.

## Project Relevance
Medium project relevance, touching layers 1 and 3 conceptually but not layers 2 or 4. The "interview" primitive — a cheap, non-binding, bilateral signal exchanged before a full application/like is committed — is structurally close to how a dating app might let users preview compatibility signals before spending a full like, directly relevant to reciprocal scoring (layer 1: learning like-back probability from partial, mutual, noisy signals). "Strategic deferral" (a firm choosing to stay vacant under uncertainty rather than being forced to accept its best current applicant) is a genuine market-design lever analogue (layer 3): it is conceptually similar to a capacity-constrained user or the platform itself pacing/holding back exposure rather than force-committing scarce reply capacity to an uncertain match. However, the paper's core technical contribution — proving *how many* interviews are needed for *learning speed* (regret) to reach a stable matching — does not address the project's central concerns at all: there is no notion of capacity skew across users (each firm has capacity exactly 1, uniformly), no exposure-redistribution or fairness objective (layer 2), no market-design levers beyond binary hire/defer (no like-limits, curated batches, or signaling), and no ecosystem-health metrics like match Gini, wasted likes, or two-sided retention (layer 4). **Disanalogy flagged:** the model's firms hire exactly one agent per round — a single-slot capacity abstraction borrowed from labor-market hiring — which does not capture a dating platform's defining problem of a small set of highly desirable users receiving *many* simultaneous likes against a hard reply-capacity ceiling; the paper's own appendix explicitly disclaims that domain-specific capacity modeling (dating included) "is beyond the scope of this work."
