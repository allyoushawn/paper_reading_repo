## Summary
On two-sided dating/matching platforms, a naive recommender that ranks receivers purely by predicted "dating rate" concentrates recommendations on a small subset of highly responsive, popular receivers, generating severe congestion that overstates true efficiency. The paper models recommendation as a many-to-many matching problem and designs *integrators* that map predicted login, like, and reciprocation ("relike") probabilities into a recommendation matrix under proposer-side cognitive-capacity constraints. It introduces *effective dates*, a congestion-discounted outcome metric, and proposes *Exposure-Constrained Deferred Acceptance* (ECDA), which caps receiver-side exposure by expected number of likes or dates rather than by headcount. Using production-grade predictive models and real interaction data from CoupLink (a Japanese dating platform with 1.5M+ cumulative users), calibrated simulations show ECDA increases effective dates and receiver-side dating probability despite reducing total (raw) dates. A large-scale geographic field experiment (difference-in-differences across Kanto vs. Kansai-Tokai) confirms the effect operates in a live platform, improving early-stage matching equity/efficiency without degrading downstream messaging engagement once extreme-tail congestion is accounted for.

## Method
- **Setting:** proposers `i ∈ [I]` (predominantly male on CoupLink) and receivers `j ∈ [J]` (predominantly female); a recommendation matrix `M = (M_ij) ∈ [0,1]` gives the probability receiver `j` is recommended to proposer `i`. Each proposer has a *cognitive capacity* `c_i` capping how many receivers he reviews (`Σ_j M_ij ≤ c_i`), treated as a hard constraint on all candidate integrators.
- **Date-formation funnel** (Figure 1): recommendation (prob. `M_ij`) → proposer logs in (prob. `λ_i`) → proposer sends a like (prob. `α_ij`, the *like rate*) → receiver logs in (prob. `λ_j`) → receiver sends a *relike* (prob. `β_ij`, the *relike rate*) → a *date* is formed. The overall probability of a date is `δ_ij M_ij`, where `δ_ij := λ_i α_ij λ_j β_ij` is the *dating rate*.
- **Performance metrics:** (1) *Average Dates* = `(1/I) Σ δ_ij M_ij`; (2) *Average Effective Dates* — discounts a proposer-receiver pair's contribution by the receiver's overall congestion `μ_j = Σ_k δ_kj M_kj` via the factor `(1 - e^{-μ_j})/μ_j`, derived under the assumption that a receiver who forms multiple dates picks exactly one uniformly at random to pursue each day (used only as a transparent congestion discount, not a behavioral claim); (3) *Dating Probability* (per proposer/receiver) — the extensive-margin probability of forming at least one date.
- **Rank-order lists (ROLs):** either "sorting by like rates" (myopic — ranks candidates only by the proposer's/receiver's own action probability) or "sorting by dating rates" (accounts for both sides' full funnel, including activity/responsiveness).
- **Integrators compared:**
  - **One-sided** — greedily recommends each proposer his top-`c_i` ROL entries with no receiver-side control (structurally close to CoupLink's actual production recommender).
  - **DA (Deferred Acceptance)** — imposes a headcount capacity `q_j` on how many proposers a receiver can be recommend-matched with; standard stable-matching DA. **Theorem 1:** when ROLs are sorted by dating rate, DA is provably equivalent to a fast greedy algorithm (sort all pairs by `δ_ij` descending, match while both sides' capacities allow), making it tractable at platform scale.
  - **ECDA (Exposure-Constrained DA)** — same DA logic, but receiver capacity `q_j` is defined on *expected exposure* rather than headcount: either expected likes received (`Σ_i M_ij λ_i α_ij ≤ q_j`, "like exposure") or expected dates formed (`Σ_i M_ij δ_ij ≤ q_j`, "date exposure"). Requires extending DA to allow fractional proposals/acceptances. **Theorem 2:** also reduces to a fast greedy algorithm when ROLs are dating-rate sorted.

## Datasets and Baselines
- **Platform:** CoupLink, a Japanese online dating service with 1.5M+ cumulative users; likes are sent primarily by men, relikes by women.
- **Predictive models:** four production gradient-boosted decision tree classifiers (proposer login, proposer like, receiver login, receiver relike), trained on registration attributes and historical usage logs, retrained daily. AUC/Brier scores (Table 1): proposer-login AUC=0.916 (Brier 0.069); proposer-like AUC=0.906 (Brier 0.100); receiver-login AUC=0.863 (Brier 0.144); receiver-relike AUC=0.800 (Brier 0.178).
- **Synthetic market simulation:** I=J=1,000 (symmetric), drawn from the joint distribution of CoupLink's actual Nov 6, 2024 predictions; proposer cognitive capacity `c_i=25`; 10 independent repeated datasets.
- **Empirical market simulation:** real proposer-receiver candidate pairs from CoupLink's production system (Kanto region, April 7, 2025), ~8,000 proposers x 5,000 receivers, `c_i=65`; used to tune parameters for the field experiment.
- **Field experiment:** geographic difference-in-differences (DiD). Treatment area = Kanto (largest population region in Japan); control area = Kansai-Tokai. Jan 13–26, 2026 (2 weeks): Kanto sessions received ECDA (dating-rate sorting, date-exposure capacity=1.5) in place of the status-quo recommender; Kansai-Tokai continued under status quo. Baseline spec: `Y_ta = α_a + δ_t + β_ITT·1{a=Kanto}·1{t∈T_deploy} + ε_ta` (area + day fixed effects); only 0.003% of users switched areas during the study window, supporting limited-interference/stable-composition identification assumptions.

## Results
**Synthetic market** (Table 2; `q_j=25` for DA unless noted): sorting by dating rates dominates sorting by like rates on every date-related metric. One-sided (like-sort): Avg Dates 0.2184, Avg Effective Dates 0.1859, Dating Prob (P/R) 0.1260/0.1716, Avg Likes 4.182. One-sided (date-sort): 0.3543 / 0.2317 / 0.1527 / 0.2237 / 3.531. DA (date-sort, `q_j=40`, best-tuned): 0.3372 / 0.2436 / 0.1495 / 0.2340 / 3.675 — DA only outperforms One-sided on effective dates and receiver dating probability once receiver capacity is loosened appropriately (default `q_j=25` DA underperforms One-sided).

**Empirical market** (Table 3, real April-2025 CoupLink data): One-sided (date-sort): Avg Dates 0.1231, Avg Eff Dates 0.0579, DatProb(P)=0.0508, DatProb(R)=0.0857, Avg Likes 3.693. CoupLink's actual current recommender: 0.1182 / 0.0584 / 0.0493 / 0.0863 / 3.565. DA (date-sort, cap=140): 0.1033 / 0.0605 / 0.0424 / 0.0904 / 3.610. **ECDA (date-exposure, cap=1.5) — best performer:** Avg Dates 0.0928, **Avg Effective Dates 0.0623 (highest)**, DatProb(P)=0.0401, **DatProb(R)=0.0932 (highest)**, Avg Likes 3.658. ECDA (like-exposure, cap=22.0): 0.1054 / 0.0603 / 0.0424 / 0.0912 / 3.308.

**Field experiment DiD estimates**, Treat×Post coefficient (Table 4, N=56 area-day observations, area+day fixed effects, SEs in parentheses; `*p<0.10, **p<0.05, ***p<0.01`; authors caution these should be read as suggestive, not definitive, since only 2 geographic macro-clusters are used):
- *(a) Predicted outcomes at recommendation:* Avg Dates **-0.003*** (0.001)**; Avg Effective Dates **+0.001** (0.000)**; DatProb(Proposer) **-0.001*** (0.000)**; DatProb(Receiver) **+0.004*** (0.001)**; AvgLikes(Receiver) **+0.415*** (0.054)**.
- *(b) Realized outcomes, full sample, within 2 weeks:* Avg Dates -0.002 (n.s.); Avg Eff Dates +0.002 (n.s.); DatProb(P) -0.001 (n.s.); DatProb(R) +0.003 (n.s.); AvgLikes(R) **+0.264*** (0.059)**.
- *(c) Realized post-engagement, full sample:* AvgMsg(Proposer) -0.002 (n.s.); AvgMsg(Receiver) **-0.004* (0.002)**; MsgProb(P) **-0.001* (0.001)**; MsgProb(R) -0.001 (n.s.) — messaging moves in the *opposite* direction from the upstream gains when the full sample is used.
- *After excluding the top 0.1% of receiver-days* (Table 5): realized Avg Effective Dates **+0.003** (0.001)**, DatProb(P) **+0.002* (0.001)**, DatProb(R) **+0.005* (0.002)**, AvgLikes(R) **+0.334*** (0.058)**; all four post-engagement (messaging) coefficients become ≈0.000 and statistically insignificant — i.e., no material harm to messaging once the extreme tail is excluded.
- **Diagnostic (Figure 6):** in the pre-period, the top 0.1% of receivers received 4.6% of all recommendations and similarly elevated shares of displays/likes, but only converted relikes to messages at roughly 1/3 the rate of other receivers (Msg(R)/Relike pass-through: 0.138 for top-0.1% vs. 0.368 for others) — these receivers dominate the funnel through the relike stage but do not follow through into substantive communication, explaining why full-sample averages mask ECDA's benefit.

## Limitations
- The paper treats predicted login/like/relike rates as ground-truth primitives for the simulation analysis; prediction error itself is explicitly set aside as a separate, well-studied ML problem, not addressed here.
- The "effective dates" congestion discount relies on an explicit simplifying assumption (a congested receiver picks one date per day uniformly at random) that the authors state is used "solely to obtain a transparent congestion discount," not as a claim about real relationship-formation behavior.
- The fast-greedy equivalence that makes DA/ECDA tractable at scale (Theorems 1–2) holds only when ROLs are sorted by dating rate; like-rate-sorted DA/ECDA are not computationally practical at the ~8,000-proposer empirical-market scale.
- Field-experiment identification rests on only two geographic macro-units (Kanto vs. Kansai-Tokai), so conventional large-cluster asymptotic standard errors are unavailable; the authors explicitly instruct readers to treat the DiD significance stars as suggestive rather than definitive.
- Full-sample realized post-engagement (messaging) outcomes move in the opposite direction from the upstream (predicted/early) gains; this tension is only resolved by excluding the top 0.1% of receiver-days, and the authors state that further improvements in *substantive* match quality (sustained messaging) "may require separate tuning or design changes beyond the ranking algorithm" — i.e., ECDA does not, by itself, solve downstream engagement quality.
- Only the receiver side is exposure-constrained in this paper's implementation; the proposer side (predominantly male on CoupLink) is capacity-constrained only via cognitive capacity `c_i`, not via a symmetric reciprocal-exposure mechanism.

## Project Relevance
One of the closest matches in the survey to all four project layers simultaneously. **Layer 1 (reciprocal scoring):** the dating rate `δ_ij = λ_i α_ij λ_j β_ij` is exactly a like-back-probability-conditioned score, decomposed into login × like × login × relike components predicted by production gradient-boosted models (AUCs 0.80–0.92). **Layer 2 (capacity-aware exposure allocation):** ECDA is a directly deployable capacity-aware allocation mechanism — receiver capacity defined on *expected likes/dates* rather than headcount is a closer match to "reply capacity" than any headcount-based DA variant, and the paper proves a fast-greedy equivalence that makes it computable at real-platform scale (8,000+ proposers); "effective dates" is directly transferable as a wasted-likes/match-quality-under-congestion metric. **Layer 3 (market-design levers):** not directly addressed — the intervention is purely an allocation/ranking mechanism; the paper does not test like limits, curated batches, or signaling. **Layer 4 (ecosystem metrics/interference-aware experimentation):** strongly addressed — the geographic DiD design explicitly reasons about cross-user interference/spillovers (a key motivation for choosing area-level over user-level randomization), and the finding that congestion concentrated in the extreme right tail (top 0.1% of receivers) mechanically distorts full-sample average post-engagement outcomes is a directly transferable lesson for interference-aware A/B testing of exposure-allocation policies in a dating market.

**Disanalogy to flag:** only the receiver side is exposure-constrained in this implementation (proposers are capacity-limited only via cognitive/attention capacity `c_i`, not via a symmetric reply-exposure cap), whereas a fully reciprocal dating market may need both sides modeled as capacity-constrained simultaneously depending on user segment (e.g., segments where the "sender" role is also desirability-skewed).
