# Two-sided market balancing for dating-app recommendation — Executive Summary

**Workplace:** `cursor-grok/` (Cursor Grok). Shared context: `../README.md`, `../requirements.md`. Notebook: `two-sided-market-balancing-dating` (`d3071ac8-16ef-4460-8991-7701679974c8`).

**The modeling target is market health under capacity limits, not single-viewer CTR/CVR.** A match needs likes from both sides. Each impression spends the viewer's attention and the shown person's reply capacity. Desirability is skewed, so a few users absorb most likes, surplus likes die, most users starve, and both sides churn.

## Design patterns (8)

1. **Reciprocal scoring, not viewer-only relevance.** Fuse P(like | viewer, candidate) with P(like-back | candidate, viewer, capacity). OkCupid trains separate voter and votee vectors because like is directed (Jablons, OkCupid Tech Blog, 2021). Harmonic mean / negative-preference filters (Pizzato et al., UMUAI 2013, RECON) and per-user reply-weighting (Kleinerman et al., RecSys 2018, RWS) are the dating-native versions. Tinder’s two-tower **P(Match)** beat **P(Like)** on market KPIs (Sokolov, Hickey, Du, Tinder Tech Blog, 2025: **+6.5% match rate**, **+22% match volume** vs +3.8% swipe-right). Hinge’s 2025 Discover ranker is the same claim in product language (who you like **and** who’s likely to like you; newsroom: **double-digit increase in matches**).
2. **Treat ranking as market-clearing with shadow prices.** CyberAgent's TU / Choo–Siow reciprocal ranker (Tomita, Togashi, Hashizume, Ohsaka, RecSys 2023) computes equilibrium matching probabilities via IPFP, then serves them with MIPS. Popularity is penalized endogenously. RecSys 2022 Tapple talk is the industry statement of the same idea.
3. **Redistribute exposure away from over-subscribed people.** LinkedIn LiJAR (Borisyuk, Zhang, Kenthapadi, KDD 2017) forecasts application volume and boosts/penalizes jobs toward min/max targets (+12% entropy, −8.7% over-served applications). Impression discounting (Lee et al., KDD 2014) is the same lever on ignored PYMK impressions (+13% invitation rate online). Kanzhun IR says the same in product language: drive traffic to **more responsive** users for “fairer” distribution (no model published).
4. **Optimize the daily assortment, not the next swipe.** Rios, Saban, Zheng (M&SOM 25(4) 2023) show recent matches make users pickier. GSB journal page: **40% more matches** vs the partner’s algorithm (simulations + field). Earlier INFORMS wording was 25%+. The PDF in the shared notebook (`dating_alf.pdf`) is a *different* manuscript (“Platform Design in Curated Dating Markets”) — do not treat it as this paper.
5. **Cap expected inbound likes, not just display count.** Fair-division / NSW ranking (Tomita and Yokoyama, RecSys 2024) cuts envy nearly to zero with a modest match-count tradeoff on dating logs. Lorenz-welfare ranking (Do, Corbett-Davies, Atif, Usunier, NeurIPS 2021) is the reciprocal-case theory. MODE (Tomita, RecSys 2026) adds mutual optimality of *direct* effects so congestion mitigation does not serve junk to individuals.
6. **Change the market rules, not only the scorer.** Application/like limits (Arnosti, Johari, Kanoria, M&SOM 2021); which side may search (Kanoria and Saban, Management Science 2021; Bumble-like “short side proposes”); restricted daily choice (Halaburda, Piskorski, Yıldırım, Management Science 2018); scarce signals (Lee and Niederle, Experimental Economics 2015, +20% accept when a rose is attached). Fong (Marketing Science 2024) shows thickening the market can *cut* matches via extra selectivity unless like limits move with size.
7. **Instrument the ecosystem, not the swipe.** Measure match Gini / inbound-like Gini (Hinge/Quartz 2017: 0.376 women vs 0.542 men; Tapple blog: recommendation Gini ~0.75), share of users with ≥1 match, wasted likes, two-sided retention. CRRS (Yang et al., KDD 2024) replaces one-sided Recall with coverage / bilateral-stability / RNDCG. MRet (Kishimoto et al., arXiv:2602.15752) argues match max and fairness are incomplete if retention is the business metric.
8. **A/B tests on two-sided markets are biased unless you design for interference.** Johari, Li, Liskovich, Weintraub (Management Science 2022): which side you randomize depends on which side is congested. UniCoRn (Nandy et al., NeurIPS 2021, LinkedIn) is how to design **producer-side** experiments when inbound outcomes depend on who viewed. Lyft MMV (Nassiri and Bright, 2025) corrects user-split estimates with dispatch shadow prices (backtest: 10% of launch decisions would flip; ~45% smaller magnitude when resources are scarce). Hayashi, Goda, Saito (RecSys 2025, Wantedly) give matching-specific OPE (DiPS/DPR) using first-stage scout/like and sparse second-stage match — still jobs logs, not dating.

## What this means for our stack

Score with a reciprocal term that conditions on the other side's remaining capacity (patterns 1–2). Tinder already A/B’d this as P(Match) vs P(Like). Allocate with an explicit inbound-capacity / redistribution layer (3–5); Tapple’s 2021 recency rerank moved conversion **+60%** after candidate-gen alone moved only likes (Ramanathan et al., AAAI 2021). Keep product levers (like caps, who initiates, signals, daily batch size, match-drip reservoirs) in the same design space as the model (6). Replace swipe-CTR north stars with match Gini, wasted-like rate, and two-sided retention (7). Do not ship capacity-aware ranking on a naive user-split A/B (8); Tapple used location-grouped tests for the same reason.

CyberAgent RecSys 2023/2024 are shared with Survey 3 — one source, two lenses; do not double-count.
