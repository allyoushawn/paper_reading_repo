## 1. Summary

**Title:** Experimental Design in Two-Sided Platforms: An Analysis of Bias  
**Authors:** Ramesh Johari, Hannah Li, Inessa Liskovich, Gabriel Y. Weintraub  
**Abstract:** Standard customer- or listing-side A/B tests can be badly biased when marketplace participants compete for finite inventory. The paper develops a dynamic marketplace model, characterizes how bias changes with supply-demand balance, and proposes two-sided randomization with interference-correction estimators.

**Key contributions:**

- Shows customer-side randomization is asymptotically unbiased in demand-constrained markets, while listing-side randomization is asymptotically unbiased in supply-constrained markets.
- Introduces two-sided randomization and TSR-Improved estimators that subtract measured cannibalization effects.
- Quantifies bias, variance, and root-mean-squared-error trade-offs against one-sided and cluster-randomized designs.

**Methodology:** A continuous-time Markov chain models Poisson customer arrivals, heterogeneous listing consideration, multinomial-logit choice, and exponential inventory replenishment. Its large-market mean-field limit supplies the true global treatment effect. Two-sided randomization assigns customers and listings independently, activating treatment only for treated pairs; TSRI estimators use cross-cell booking-rate differences as interference corrections.

**Main results:** In simulations with 5,000 listings and 500 runs, customer- and listing-side designs each become nearly unbiased only in the appropriate market extreme. At balanced demand, TSRI-2 lowers absolute bias from about 20–22% to about 8% of the global treatment effect, but its normalized standard error rises to about 0.19 versus 0.08–0.10 for one-sided designs.

## 2. Experiment Critique

**Design:** The mean-field model provides a known estimand, and simulations compare customer-side, listing-side, naive two-sided, improved two-sided, and cluster designs across market balance. This cleanly isolates interference, but behavior is generated by the assumed queueing and choice model.

**Statistical validity:** Each setting uses 500 simulations with bootstrapped 95th-percentile confidence intervals. Bias, standard error, and root mean squared error are normalized by the global treatment effect. No real-platform validation is provided.

**Online experiments:** None. Simulations use 5,000 listings, 25 time units, and a five-unit burn-in.

**Reproducibility:** Model equations and simulation settings are specified. Public code is not specified in source.

**Overall:** The evidence supports the market-balance-dependent bias claims within the model. TSRI-2's variance penalty, sensitivity to choice assumptions, and lack of valid interference-aware confidence intervals limit direct deployment claims.

## 3. Industry Contribution

**Deployability:** Two-sided eligibility cells are operationally plausible when treatment can be activated only for treated customer-listing pairs. Some whole-ranking interventions cannot be cleanly localized this way.

**Problems solved:** Biased estimates caused by cannibalization and competition in two-sided marketplace experiments.

**Engineering cost:** Moderate to high: dual-side assignment, cell-level logging, market-balance estimation, and custom estimators are required.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

**Paper's claimed novelty:** A dynamic inventory model linking experimental bias to market balance, plus two-sided designs and estimators that explicitly correct competing spillovers.

**Prior work comparison:** Blake and Coey (2014), Fradkin (2015/2019), and Holtz et al. (2020) establish marketplace interference; Bajari et al. develop multiple randomization; Ugander et al. (2013) and Athey et al. (2018) study clustered/network interference; Kurtz (1970) underpins the mean-field limit.

**Verification:** The novelty comparison is limited to the source-scoped introduction and related work; no external forward-citation search was performed.

## Project Relevance

**Exact mechanism:** Randomize both viewers and shown users, activate treatment only for treated pairs, and estimate spillover through outcome differences in untreated cross-cells. TSRI-2 subtracts those cannibalization terms with weights based on market balance.

**Metrics and reported effect:** At balanced demand, normalized absolute bias falls from roughly 0.20–0.22 for customer-, listing-, or naive two-sided estimators to roughly 0.08 for TSRI-2. Its normalized standard error is about 0.19 versus 0.08–0.10 for one-sided estimators.

**Capacity/congestion relevance:** Capacity is binary listing occupancy with exponential replenishment. That captures competition for scarce availability, but not reciprocal likes, parallel conversations, or graded reply capacity.

**Practical mapping:** Use the framework to diagnose whether viewer-side or profile-side randomization is safer and to instrument cross-cell spillover. A dating implementation would need receiver-specific attention capacity and match/conversation outcomes.

**Dating fit: Medium.** The experimentation logic transfers directly, but the unilateral booking model does not represent double opt-in matching.
