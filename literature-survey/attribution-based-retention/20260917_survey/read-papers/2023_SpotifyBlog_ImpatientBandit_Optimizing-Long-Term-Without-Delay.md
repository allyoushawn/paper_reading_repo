# Optimizing for the Long-Term Without Delay (Impatient Bandits)

**Source:** https://research.atspotify.com/2023/07/optimizing-for-the-long-term-without-delay | **NLM source id:** f9864eeb-df64-4530-9ca6-92d2063153a9 | **Year / venue:** 2023, Spotify Research blog (underlying paper: McDonald et al., KDD 2023, "Impatient Bandits: Optimizing for the Long-Term Without Delay") | **Affiliation:** Spotify Research | **Analyzed:** 2026-09-17 (NotebookLM, 2 queries)

## 1. Summary
Optimizing recommenders for short-term proxies (clicks, streams) misaligns with long-term satisfaction/retention, but optimizing directly for long-term outcomes (e.g., 60-day podcast engagement) creates an item cold-start problem: new content must wait a long delay before its true value is known, forcing a tradeoff between alignment and learning velocity. Contribution: the "Impatient Bandit" framework, which uses progressively-revealed intermediate outcomes ("trace" z_1...z_K observed at increasing delays) correlated with the final long-term reward, combined with a Bayesian filter (updating a Gaussian belief over the expected trace as data arrives) and Thompson Sampling for exploration/exploitation. **Unit of credit:** per candidate item/action (e.g., which podcast show to recommend) — not per user touch. **Outcome optimized:** long-term cumulative engagement reward r (days engaged with a newly discovered show over 60 days), modeled as a weighted function of the intermediate trace. **Bias/confounding handling:** not selection-bias correction in the project's sense — the paper works in a non-contextual bandit setting that explicitly integrates out individual user-level variance/heterogeneity to estimate a population-level expected trace per item; it does not address the "engaged users interact more" confound at all.

## 2. Evidence
Simulation-based evaluation on a pool of 200 podcast shows using real-world Spotify podcast discovery and 60-day engagement logs; hundreds of simulations comparing cumulative regret across four reward models: (1) Delayed baseline (waits full 60 days), (2) Short-term proxy, (3) Oracle (unachievable, immediate full reward), (4) Impatient Bandit (proposed). Results are qualitative/graphical rather than tabulated: strong correlation observed between 10–20 day engagement traces and 60-day total engagement; the short-term proxy learns fast but plateaus with non-zero asymptotic regret (misalignment); the naive 60-day delayed baseline accumulates high regret from slow feedback; the Impatient Bandit's regret curve tracks close to the Oracle's, substantially beating both other baselines.

## 3. Limitations
Short-term proxies enable fast learning but plateau due to imperfect alignment with the 60-day outcome. The naive fully-delayed baseline is too slow for practical content exploration. The non-contextual simplification integrates out user-level heterogeneity, explicitly placing personalized multi-touch user modeling outside the paper's scope. The Bayesian filter's historical-transfer approach depends on the assumption that intermediate-to-long-term correlations learned from past content releases remain stable for new content — an assumption that could fail if content/behavior patterns shift.

## 5. Project Relevance
- **Answers:** neither directly — this is about the cold-start/delayed-reward learning problem for ranking new items, not attribution of an outcome to which past user actions caused it; only tangentially related to Q2 via its "intermediate outcome as an early signal of a delayed outcome" idea.

- **Transferable components:** the core "trace" idea — using early post-swipe/post-match signals (e.g., day-1 to day-3 activity) as a leading indicator to predict N7 retention sooner, reducing label-delay/cold-start pain for a ranking model that needs N7 labels quickly; Bayesian belief updating as data progressively arrives could inform how a candidate-scoring model updates its estimate of a swipe's eventual retention value before the full 7-day window completes.

- **Transfer rating:** background — useful only as a label-timing/cold-start idea (early signals predict delayed N7 outcome), not as an attribution or credit-assignment method; the project's core need (fractional per-swipe credit, selection-bias correction) is out of scope for this paper.
