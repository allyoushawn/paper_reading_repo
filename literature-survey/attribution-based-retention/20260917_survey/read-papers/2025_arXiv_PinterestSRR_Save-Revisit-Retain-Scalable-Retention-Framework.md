# Save, Revisit, Retain: A Scalable Framework for Enhancing User Retention in Large-Scale Recommender Systems

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18013.pdf | **NLM source id:** f7f34e82-7545-4be7-885a-8bdb87041b12 | **Year / venue:** 2025, arXiv 2511.18013 (AAAI 2026) | **Affiliation:** Pinterest Inc. | **Analyzed:** 2026-09-17 (NotebookLM, 2 queries)

## 1. Summary
User retention on Pinterest is tied to "revisitation" — a user returning to view previously saved content. Two challenges: (1) attribution — confounders (content quality, UI, notifications, shifting intent) make it hard to know which action triggered a revisit; (2) scale — revisits happen days/weeks later, requiring joining massive cross-surface, cross-session histories. Contribution: a lightweight surrogate-attribution framework on Pinterest's Related Pins surface (~50% of platform traffic, 500M+ users). **Unit of credit:** per candidate Pin (item-level, not fractional across a sequence). **Outcome attributed/optimized:** a surrogate joint label — save (repin) followed by revisit (impression or grid-click) within a strict 0–6 day window, feeding a new auxiliary head in an existing multi-task ranking model (MMoE + DCNv2), which ultimately targets 28-day active days / DAU / time spent. **Bias/confounding handling:** not causal inference in the treatment-effect sense — it is a deterministic surrogate-attribution rule: a revisit is credited to the saved item only if the user returns to that exact same item on their own profile within the window; unrelated return visits are excluded. Historical popularity features log both action volume and unique-user counts, specifically to prevent a single hyperactive user from skewing item-level popularity signals (a lightweight de-biasing step, not full selection-bias correction).

## 2. Evidence
Offline: 27 days training (~6.6B examples), 1 day calibration, 3 days eval (~700M examples). Revisitation head vs. baseline MMoE+DCNv2: +40.15% NDCG@3, +43.44% MAP@3, +35.40% Recall@3, +21.62% pairwise accuracy; overall Hits@3 +0.59% (repin) and +0.65% (revisit) with no tradeoffs on other heads. Online A/B (2 months, 24M users): Active Users +0.10% volume / +0.08% propensity (p<0.05); revisitation metrics +0.65–1.42%; repin +0.94% volume; sessions ≥5min +0.41%; time spent on Related Pins +0.53%, own profile +0.68%, total app +0.39%. Baseline: existing production MMoE+DCNv2 model predicting grid-click, save, outbound click, long-click only.

## 3. Limitations
Label sparsity — highly active users repin far more than low-activity users, leaving limited revisitation labels for low-activity cohorts. Diversity-vs-engagement tension: past diversity-boosting experiments reduced click/save by >1%. Negative repin lift on low-historical-revisitation topics (Finance, Health, Quotes) as the model shifted exposure toward high-revisitation topics (Beauty, Architecture, Entertainment) — a fairness/coverage side effect. Save rate and revisit rate can diverge (e.g., DIY/Parenting/Health have high saves but low revisits), showing immediate action ≠ downstream retention signal.

## 5. Project Relevance
- **Answers:** Q2 (partly — direct empirical example of attributing retention-relevant behavior to an individual saved item, though not fractional across a full interaction sequence); not really Q1.
- **Attributes retention to individual interactions?** partly — attributes a specific surrogate outcome (same-item revisit within 0–6 days) to a specific save action, but does not attribute the ultimate DAU/retention outcome fractionally across a full multi-touch history; it is single-touch (save → its own revisit), not multi-touch credit splitting.
- **Transferable components:** the surrogate-attribution pattern (link a candidate-level action to a well-defined, temporally-bounded downstream event) as a template for defining swipe→match/message surrogate labels; adding an auxiliary head to an existing multi-task ranker at zero serving cost; unique-user normalization of popularity features to dampen power-user skew.
- **Required changes:** extend from a single action-pair surrogate (save→revisit on the same item) to a full heterogeneous funnel (swipe→match→message→return) and to fractional credit across multiple candidates rather than a strict same-item link; move from "did this exact item get revisited" to "was next-7-day app-level retention driven by this swipe," which requires distributing credit across many swipes rather than a 1:1 item attribution.
- **On last-touch:** not explicitly discussed; the paper implicitly critiques systems that focus only on immediate in-session engagement (clicks/saves) and ignore multi-day return dynamics, and notes a prior click→revisit two-model approach (Zhang et al. 2021) was too costly to serve — motivating their integrated single-model approach instead.
- **Transfer rating:** adaptable — the surrogate-attribution and zero-cost multi-task-head pattern transfer well, but the method is single-touch item-level attribution, not the fractional multi-touch credit splitting the project needs.
