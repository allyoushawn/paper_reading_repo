**Date analyzed:** 2026-08-17

## 1. Summary

Title: See Beyond a Single View: Multi-Attribution Learning Leads to Better Conversion Rate Prediction. Authors: Sishuo Chen, Zhangming Chan, Xiang-Rong Sheng, Lei Zhang, Sheng Chen, Chenghuan Hou, Han Zhu, Jian Xu, Bo Zheng. Affiliation: Taobao & Tmall Group of Alibaba, Beijing, China. Venue: CIKM '25 (34th ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management), Seoul, Republic of Korea, November 10-14, 2025.

Abstract/contribution: industrial CVR-prediction models are conventionally trained on labels from a single, system-optimized attribution mechanism (typically Last-Click), which discards signal from alternative attribution perspectives (First-Click, Linear, data-driven Multi-Touch Attribution/MTA) that also describe how conversion credit is distributed across a user's ad-click touchpoints. The paper proposes MAL (Multi-Attribution Learning): a joint framework with (1) an Attribution Knowledge Aggregator (AKA), a multi-task learner with one prediction tower per attribution mechanism, each trained on that mechanism's fractional-credit label via weighted binary cross-entropy (the attribution weight serves as the sample's importance weight), producing per-mechanism "conversion knowledge vectors" concatenated into a knowledge embedding K; and (2) a Primary Target Predictor (PTP), the deployed CVR model for the system's primary attribution target, which fuses K (via an MLP projection + element-wise addition) with its own features to improve prediction on the primary label. A third component, CAT (Cartesian-based Auxiliary Training), adds an auxiliary classification task over the Cartesian product of all N binary attribution labels (2^N = 16 classes for the paper's 4 mechanisms) to further enrich AKA's supervision.

Methodology: all components share a bottom (embedding + feature-interaction) architecture. Compared against a production baseline (Base: SimTier multimodal feature extraction + attention-based user-behavior modules) and multi-task-learning baselines (Shared-Bottom, MMoE, PLE, HoME) reimplemented with each attribution label as an independent auxiliary task. Evaluated with two groups of experiments, using Last-Click and MTA respectively as the primary target. Offline data: two months of Taobao display-advertising click logs for training, the following day's logs for test (real production data, not public).

Main results: offline, MAL beats Base by +0.51% GAUC / +0.14% AUC (Last-Click primary target) and +0.75% GAUC / +0.21% AUC (MTA primary target), and beats the best MTL baseline by +0.20%/+0.15% GAUC and +0.05%/+0.06% AUC respectively across the two target settings. Ablations show AKA alone contributes +0.38% GAUC/+0.14% AUC, and CAT adds a further +0.13% GAUC on top of AKA; a parameter-count control experiment (all towers trained on the primary label only) shows the gains come from the *information* in diverse attribution labels rather than from added model parameters. Online: a strict A/B test on Taobao's display-advertising system (May 21-25, 2025) shows MAL delivers +2.7% GMV, +1.2% BuyCnt, and +2.6% ROI versus the production baseline.

## 2. Experiment Critique

Design: offline comparison is controlled (same shared-bottom base, same feature set, one-epoch training for fairness across MTL baselines) and includes an ablation study isolating AKA vs. CAT contributions and a parameter-count control (Conjecture I vs. Conjecture II analysis) to rule out the confound that MAL's gains simply come from having more parameters than the single-attribution baseline. Statistical validity: no confidence intervals, standard deviations, or significance tests are reported for any offline GAUC/AUC delta or the online A/B percentages (the online test is a single 5-day run, single arm vs. control). Online experiment: a real production A/B test (a strength relative to some papers in this corpus), but the 5-day window is short and no statistical-significance test is reported for the GMV/BuyCnt/ROI lifts. Reproducibility: architecture and loss formulas are given in detail (Eq. 1-2, Algorithm 1 for CAT labels), but the dataset is proprietary Taobao production data; no code release is mentioned.

## 3. Industry Contribution

This is an industrial deployment paper: MAL retains PTP's architecture as the production CVR model to "ensure compatibility with existing infrastructure and deployment pipelines," and the paper frames AKA as auxiliary-only, with only the fused knowledge vector K feeding PTP — but the paper does not explicitly state whether AKA's towers must still run at inference time to produce K for PTP's fusion step, or are discarded after training; this serving-latency detail is left unclear. The paper reports a validated online business outcome (+2.6% ROI, +2.7% GMV in a live A/B test), described by the authors as substantial for their ad platform. Engineering cost: requires access to labels from multiple attribution mechanisms (Last-Click, First-Click, Linear, MTA) simultaneously for the same training data, which presupposes the underlying attribution-computation pipeline (including causal-inference-based MTA models such as DeepMTA/CausalMTA/LiDDA, cited but not implemented in this paper) already exists and produces fractional-credit labels.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

The paper states it is the first to explore a "multi-attribution learning" framework for CVR prediction — i.e., the first to mine auxiliary training signal from *multiple* attribution-mechanism labels jointly rather than training exclusively on the single production-critical attribution mechanism (citing prior CVR work such as ESMM, ESCM2, DFM, and delayed-feedback and multi-task CVR literature as all confined to single-attribution training). It differs from general multi-touch-attribution (MTA) research (DeepMTA, CAMTA, CausalMTA, LiDDA — causal-inference models for computing attribution weights) by treating attribution-label computation as a given upstream input and instead studying how to fuse multiple already-computed attribution views inside the CVR ranking model itself. It also cites Alibaba's own related work: Zhu et al. 2017, "Optimized Cost per Click in Taobao Display Advertising" (Paper 3 of this batch), as part of the platform's advertising-infrastructure context, and KEEP (Zhang et al. 2022) as the design inspiration for PTP's knowledge-fusion mechanism.

## Project Relevance

Speaks narrowly to **Q2** (attribution of a user-level, delayed outcome to an item-level decision) only insofar as it demonstrates one industrial pattern for using multiple credit-assignment views as auxiliary training signal inside a CVR ranking model — the specific mechanism (multi-touch ad-click attribution) is explicitly excluded from this survey's scope per the project README, so its relevance is confined to the *auxiliary-multi-view-as-training-signal* architectural pattern (AKA/PTP knowledge fusion, CAT Cartesian auxiliary task) rather than to attribution theory itself. **Low relevance to Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7, Q8** — the paper does not make retention/LTV a training objective, does not discuss delayed-label horizons or censoring, does not combine short- and long-term value heads, does not address incrementality, and has no two-sided/reciprocal-market structure; its offline/online evaluation (AUC/GAUC + A/B on GMV/BuyCnt/ROI) is a fairly standard CVR-model evaluation design without novelty for delayed or noisy long-horizon effects.
