# Paper Analysis: PROXIMA: Proxy Metric Validation with Segment-Level Fragility Detection for Online Controlled Experiments

**Source:** `/Users/fox/Projects/paper_reading_repo/literature-survey/unified-ltv-ranking-dating/claude_opus/pdfs/2604.14352.pdf`
**Date analyzed:** 2026-08-17

## 1. Summary

Avinash Amudala (Rochester Institute of Technology), arXiv, April 2026, "conducted as independent research" (single author, not affiliated with a company or lab team, per the acknowledgments). PROXIMA is a diagnostic framework for auditing whether a candidate **proxy metric** (a fast, easily-measured signal such as click-through rate or early session count) reliably substitutes for a slow-to-measure long-term outcome (retention, lifetime revenue) in online controlled experiments (A/B tests). Its central observation is that a proxy can be positively correlated with the long-term outcome *in aggregate* while pointing the wrong direction *within specific user segments* — a Simpson's-Paradox-style failure mode the aggregate correlation masks. PROXIMA scores a proxy on three dimensions, all normalized to [0,1] and combined into a weighted **Composite Reliability Score R**: (1) normalized Pearson correlation between the proxy's and the long-term outcome's experiment-level treatment effects across a historical corpus of A/B tests; (2) directional accuracy (DA) — the fraction of experiments where the proxy's and the outcome's treatment-effect signs agree; (3) segment-level fragility rate (FR) — the fraction of (experiment, segment) pairs where the segment-level proxy effect sign contradicts the *global* long-term effect sign. A companion decision-simulation module compares proxy-driven ship/no-ship decisions against an oracle with perfect long-term knowledge (win rate, false-positive/negative rate, regret). Validated on 80 simulated A/B tests built from two public datasets: Criteo Uplift (13.98M observations, advertising) and KuaiRec (7,176 users, video recommendation), achieving composite reliability 0.80 (Criteo) and 0.62 (KuaiRec), with 98.4% average agreement with the oracle decision across both domains despite the two domains showing very different segment-level fragility (13% vs. 68%).

## 2. Experiment Critique

Both datasets are *not* real historical A/B test corpora: they are single-trial (or fully-observed, non-experimental) datasets that the author converts into 50 and 30 "simulated" experiments by random partitioning of users, a limitation the author explicitly names in Section 6.5 ("this approach ... does not fully capture the variation of real experiment corpora"). The composite score's weights (0.6, 0.2, 0.2) are fixed heuristically, not learned or cross-validated — also disclosed as a limitation, along with the absence of multiple-testing correction for segment-level sign-flip detection (meaning some flagged "fragile" segments could be false positives from noise, especially with many segments). No direct comparison against the surrogate-index method (Athey et al. 2019) is performed, again for a stated, disclosed reason (no held-out corpus with both surrogates and long-term outcomes available). Effect sizes are reported as small-to-medium (Cohen's d = 0.15 Criteo, 0.22 KuaiRec) with Welch's t-test significance in 96% (Criteo) / 90% (KuaiRec) of experiments — reasonably transparent statistical reporting for a solo-author preprint. This is an unreviewed arXiv preprint (April 2026, no venue), so none of these disclosed limitations have been vetted by peer review.

## 3. Industry Contribution

Directly built as a practitioner tool: lightweight (author reports the full Criteo pipeline — 13.9M rows, 50 experiments, 4 proxies, 12 segments — runs in ~5 minutes on a 16-core laptop), with a described architecture exposing a REST API, a React dashboard, and CLI tools (Figure 1), and open-sourced code/reproduction scripts on GitHub. Requires only experiment-level treatment effects and segment labels as input — no new data collection beyond what a standard A/B testing pipeline already produces. The paper explicitly frames its five practical guidelines (Section 6.4: validate before trusting, prioritize directional accuracy over correlation magnitude, tolerate FR<0.70 if DA is high, monitor reliability over time, use fragility profiles as guardrail metrics) as directly actionable for an experimentation team.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

Explicitly positioned as complementary to, not competing with, the surrogate-index literature (Athey et al. 2019; Zhang et al. 2023, Netflix's 200-A/B-test surrogate-index evaluation): those methods answer "what is the long-term effect, given short-term data," while PROXIMA answers "if I use this proxy to decide whether to ship, how often will I be wrong, and where." Distinguished from segment-level heterogeneous-treatment-effect tools (causal forests, meta-learners, Statsig's Differential Impact Detection, Netflix's `sherlock` library, the "De-paradox Tree") by testing whether the *proxy–outcome directional relationship* (not a single outcome) is heterogeneous across segments — i.e., it audits an existing proxy choice rather than discovering which segments benefit most from treatment.

## Project Relevance

Directly relevant to **Q6** (evaluation under slow, noisy retention effects and heterogeneity) and provides a concrete, low-cost method for the survey's evaluation-plan deliverable (Synthesis deliverable 6): segment-level proxy validation before trusting any short-term signal as a stand-in for retention/revenue. Also a rare positive example for the survey's prediction-vs-incrementality distinction (**field 6 above**) — unlike most RL-for-retention papers in this corpus, PROXIMA's entire method operates on estimated treatment effects rather than predicted outcomes. **Low relevance for Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5, Q7, Q8** — it is not a ranking model, has no item-level credit-assignment mechanism, and does not address two-sided-market structure.
