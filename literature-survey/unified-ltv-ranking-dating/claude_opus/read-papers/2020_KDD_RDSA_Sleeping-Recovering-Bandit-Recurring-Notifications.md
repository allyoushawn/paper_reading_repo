# Paper Analysis: A Sleeping, Recovering Bandit Algorithm for Optimizing Recurring Notifications

**Source:** KDD 2020 (ACM DOI 10.1145/3394486.3403351)
**Date analyzed:** 2026-08-16

## 1. Summary

Yancey and Settles (Duolingo) address a production bandit problem, not a ranking problem: which notification template to send a user on each recurring reminder round, to maximize engagement, under two conditions that break standard multi-armed bandit assumptions. First, **sleeping arms** — many templates carry hard eligibility rules (e.g., "must have a 3+ day streak"), and standard bandits assume all arms are always available. Eligibility acts as a confounder: a template restricted to already-highly-engaged users will show an inflated average reward regardless of its actual causal efficacy, so naive reward comparison across arms with different eligibility criteria is invalid. Second, **novelty decay / recovering arms** — fresh, recently-unseen templates get a reward boost purely from novelty; a standard bandit that ignores this will converge onto repeating one "best" template, which desensitizes users and degrades long-run engagement.

The paper's contribution is the **Recovering Difference Softmax Algorithm**, which solves both problems together. To control for eligibility confounding, it computes each arm's score as a *relative difference* — the reward when the arm is used (µ⁺) minus the reward when the arm is eligible but a different arm is chosen instead (µ⁻), both estimated via weighted importance sampling over the arm's own historical eligible rounds — which the authors state "effectively controls for the effects of the eligibility criteria on the expected reward." Because small-sample arms have high-variance score estimates (which can cause degenerate runaway behavior), the score is regularized via empirical-Bayes shrinkage toward a Beta-prior global mean, weighted by Kish's effective sample size. To counter novelty decay, a cognitively-motivated exponential-decay recency penalty (modeled on human memory fading) is subtracted from the score based on days since the arm was last shown to that user. Arms are selected each round via softmax over the resulting adjusted score. The system is deployed as a full production architecture — a streaming Decision Log, a key-value User Arm History store, a daily-batch Scorer, and an online Arm Selector — optimizing millions of daily Duolingo reminder notifications. A two-week production A/B test against a years-optimized uniform-random baseline delivered +0.5% Daily Active Users, +0.4% lessons completed, and +2.0% new-user D7 retention (all statistically significant, p<0.05).

## 2. Experiment Critique

**Design.** An offline log-data experiment using 34 days of historical uniform-random-selection logs (15-day train / 19-day test split, 88M / 114M rows), evaluated via off-policy weighted-importance-sampling estimation across candidate policy variants (UI-language granularity, recency-penalty grid search, softmax-temperature sweep), followed by a genuine online production A/B test.

**Statistical validity.** Online A/B results are explicitly marked statistically significant (p<0.05) for most reported metrics — a level of rigor not matched by the other three papers in this batch. Offline off-policy estimates are reported with explicit uncertainty bounds (e.g., ±0.00015 for reward estimates, a ±0.15-percentage-point credible-interval target for arm-history truncation), and the empirical-Bayes shrinkage hyperparameter (σ) is itself estimated from data rather than hand-tuned, with its necessity explicitly quantified: without shrinkage, 38% of small-sample arms show scores outside ±5%, which the authors state "could cause highly degenerate behavior in the bandit."

**Online experiments.** A genuine two-week randomized production A/B test against a strong, multi-year-optimized uniform-random baseline, followed by an ongoing 5% random holdout introduced five months post-launch specifically to detect silent regressions — one of the more disciplined online-monitoring designs in this survey.

**Reproducibility.** Full mathematical specification of the importance-sampling estimators, empirical-Bayes shrinkage, recency-decay formula, and softmax selection policy, plus a full production-system architecture diagram (decision logger, arm-history store, scorer, arm selector) in the appendix. The dataset (Duolingo's proprietary notification logs) is not public and no code release is mentioned.

**Overall.** One of the strongest experimental designs in this batch — explicit online significance testing, uncertainty-quantified offline off-policy estimation, and an ongoing holdout-based regression monitor — though, like the industry papers in this batch, all headline numbers come from one company's proprietary deployment.

## 3. Industry Contribution

The paper documents a complete production architecture: a streaming Decision Log (flat files, high write volume, never updated once written), a key-value User Arm History store (tracks last-shown time per arm per user, feeding the recency penalty), a daily-batch Scorer job (joins decision logs with event data to update arm scores), and an online Arm Selector (reads scores and history, computes softmax probabilities, draws and logs the decision). The system is explicitly designed so new templates require no special handling — a new arm's score simply starts at 0, and softmax exploration naturally samples it until enough data accrues. The batch-scoring design (arm scores updated once daily, not after every decision) is a stated latency/engineering tradeoff: because scores change slowly day-to-day, batch updates "greatly simplify" the update process and improve scalability, at the cost of a ~1-day feedback delay that the empirical-Bayes shrinkage specifically mitigates (otherwise a newly-introduced bad template could dominate selection for a full day before correction). The paper also documents an operational monitoring pattern largely absent from the other three papers in this batch: a permanent 5% random holdout, added five months after launch, purely to detect silent bugs or regressions in an already-live bandit.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

The claimed novelty is a practical, unified bandit variant solving the sleeping-arms (conditional eligibility) and recovering-arms (novelty decay) problems simultaneously at industrial scale, validated with both offline and online real-world evaluation — the authors explicitly contrast this against most bandit literature, which "focuses mainly on proving theoretical performance bounds." Prior work discussed: **Kleinberg, Niculescu-Mizil & Sharma, "Regret bounds for sleeping experts and bandits," Machine Learning 2010** — the sleeping-bandits theoretical framework (an exp4-based solution over arm orderings), which the authors note is intractable at their scale (|A|! possible orderings) and not obviously compatible with recovering-bandit solutions. **Pike-Burke & Grunewalder, "Recovering Bandits," NeurIPS 2019** — the recovering-bandits framework, whose Gaussian-process solution the authors explicitly reject in favor of their simpler two-parameter exponential-decay formula, on the grounds that Gaussian processes would need far more data than their setting provides. **Robbins, "Some aspects of the sequential design of experiments," 1952** — the foundational multi-armed bandit formulation. **Sutton & Barto, "Reinforcement learning: An introduction," 2018** — source of the softmax policy and behavior-policy terminology used throughout. **Ebbinghaus, "Memory: a contribution to experimental psychology," 1885**, and **Settles & Meeder, "A trainable spaced repetition model for language learning," ACL 2016** — the cognitive-science basis for using an exponential-decay function to model the fading of the recency penalty.

## Project Relevance

**This paper does not address ranking, and this survey does not stretch it to fit that frame.** Its relevance is narrower and specific: it is a production system that optimizes a **recurring intervention** (which notification to send) against a **long-term retention outcome** (D1/D7 retention, DAU) under delayed feedback, small-sample arms, and non-stationary rewards — conditions structurally identical to what the dating app's unified model will face once retention becomes the direct training objective. It speaks most directly to **Q6** (evaluation under slow, noisy, delayed retention signals: the paper's off-policy importance-sampling evaluation plus its permanent random holdout for ongoing regression detection is a directly reusable evaluation pattern) and partially to **Q3** (label definition — its binary, short-window reward and adaptive recency-truncated training window is a concrete example of trading off label freshness against sample-size stability). Its relative-difference, eligibility-confound-controlled scoring method (see field 6 above) is also a useful, lightweight precedent for **Q5** (uplift/incremental framing), even though it falls short of a full causal-inference treatment.

**Low-to-moderate relevance to the survey's central ranking-migration question.** It does not address **Q2** (no item/slate credit assignment beyond single-arm attribution), **Q4** (no fusion of short-term and long-term heads — there is only one reward signal), **Q7** (no two-sided, reciprocal, or congestion treatment — Duolingo notifications are a one-sided platform-to-user intervention), or **Q8** (no migration narrative from a CTR-style model to a unified LTV model — this is a bandit replacing a uniform-random baseline, not a ranking-objective migration).
