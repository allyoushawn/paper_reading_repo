# Paper Analysis: Integrating Predictive Models into Two-Sided Recommendations: A Matching-Theoretic Approach

**Source:** arXiv:2602.19689 (2026-02-24), econ.GN. University of Tokyo, MiDATA Co., Ltd., LINKBAL Co., Ltd. `/Users/fox/Projects/paper_reading_repo/literature-survey/unified-ltv-ranking-dating/claude_opus/pdfs/2602.19689.pdf`
**Date analyzed:** 2026-08-17

## 1. Summary

Kazuki Sekiya, Suguru Otani, Yuki Komatsu, Sachio Ohkawa, and Shunya Noda (University of Tokyo, with MiDATA Co. and LINKBAL Co.) address exactly the seam this survey batch is looking for: how to take an existing suite of behavioral **prediction** models — login, like, and reciprocation ("relike") probabilities — and integrate them into a **matching-theoretic** recommendation mechanism that controls receiver-side congestion. Their setting is CoupLink, a Japanese online dating platform with over 1.5 million cumulative users, where a "date" requires a full chain of events: the platform recommends receiver j to proposer i (probability M_ij, the platform's only control variable) → proposer logs in (λ_i) → proposer likes j (α_ij) → receiver logs in (λ_j) → receiver relikes i (β_ij). The product δ_ij = λ_iα_ijλ_jβ_ij is the "dating rate." CoupLink's status-quo mechanism (termed **One-sided** in the paper) simply ranks receivers by this dating rate and shows each proposer his top receivers up to a cognitive-capacity limit c_i — a purely proposer-side ranking with no constraint on how many proposers a single popular receiver is shown to, which the authors show generates severe receiver-side congestion: a small subset of highly responsive receivers is repeatedly recommended to many proposers.

**The joining mechanism.** The four component behavioral rates (proposer login, proposer like, receiver login, receiver relike) are each predicted by CoupLink's production gradient-boosted-tree binary classifiers (AUC 0.916 / 0.906 / 0.863 / 0.800 respectively; Brier scores 0.069 / 0.100 / 0.144 / 0.178) and are **treated as accurate, known primitives** — the paper explicitly brackets prediction quality as solved and out of scope. What the paper contributes is the *integrator*: a mechanism that maps these four predicted rates, together with each proposer's cognitive capacity, into a recommendation matrix. They formalize this as a **many-to-many matching problem** and adapt the **deferred acceptance (DA)** algorithm — the classic stable-matching mechanism — to this setting, with two key departures from textbook DA: (1) receiver "capacity" need not be a headcount at all; and (2) because login/like/relike rates are fractional probabilities rather than binary preferences, DA itself must be extended to allow fractional proposals and acceptances.

**Exposure-Constrained Deferred Acceptance (ECDA).** The paper's central proposed method redefines a receiver's capacity not as "how many proposers she is matched with" (the textbook DA formulation, termed **DA** here) but as an **exposure quota** on the *expected number of likes or dates* she receives — i.e., Σ_i M_ijλ_iα_ij ≤ q_j ("like exposure") or Σ_i M_ijδ_ij ≤ q_j ("date exposure"). The justification is behavioral: receivers cannot observe how many proposers they were recommend-matched to, only how many likes and dates they actually receive, so capacity should be defined on the variable that actually drives the receiver's experience and downstream congestion. The authors also introduce **effective dates**, a congestion-discounted match-quality metric: a receiver who accrues μ_j expected dates in a day is assumed to develop only one of them into a deeper relationship (selected uniformly at random), giving an effective dating rate δ*_ij = [(1−e^(−μⱼ))/μⱼ]·δ_ij that discounts a date's value by the receiver's own congestion level. Two theorems show that when rank-order lists are sorted by dating rate, both DA and ECDA reduce to a simple, fast greedy pass over pairs sorted by δ_ij — making both mechanisms scalable to production.

**Validation.** Calibrated simulations on both a synthetic market (I=J=1,000, built from CoupLink's real November 2024 predicted-rate distributions) and an empirical market (~8,000 proposers × 5,000 receivers, built from CoupLink's real April 2025 candidate pairs in the Kanto region) show ECDA with date-exposure achieves the best average effective dates and receiver-side dating probability among all integrators tested, outperforming both One-sided and a naively capacity-constrained DA. A large-scale geographic difference-in-differences field experiment (Kanto treated vs. Kansai-Tokai control, January 13–26, 2026) deploying ECDA (date-exposure, capacity 1.5) at production scale confirms the simulation's predicted-outcome direction with statistical significance, and — after excluding the top 0.1% of receiver-days (an extreme-congestion tail that converts likes to messages very poorly) — shows statistically significant realized gains in effective dates, dating probability, and average likes, without harming downstream messaging for the remaining 99.9% of users.

## 2. Experiment Critique

**Design.** Rare among papers in this batch for combining (1) calibrated simulation using real production prediction outputs (not hypothetical relevance scores), (2) two theoretical greedy-equivalence results that make the proposed mechanism production-scalable, and (3) an actual live field experiment at platform scale. The synthetic-market simulation is used specifically to test the like-rate-sorted variant of DA/ECDA, which is not computationally tractable at empirical-market scale (no greedy shortcut exists for that sort order) — a methodologically honest acknowledgment that their own fast-implementation results depend on sorting by dating rate specifically.

**Statistical validity.** The field experiment's identification rests on a difference-in-differences design with only **two geographic macro-units** (Kanto vs. Kansai-Tokai), which the authors themselves flag: "standard inference is unreliable with only two geographic clusters," and they explicitly state that reported significance stars should be read as "suggestive rather than definitive." They substitute event-study pre-trend visual inspection for formal cluster-robust standard errors. Assumptions underlying the DID design (parallel trends, no anticipation, stable composition, limited interference) are each separately discussed and partly supported by auxiliary evidence (e.g., only 0.003% of users ever switched areas during the study window, supporting limited interference/stable composition).

**Online experiments.** Yes — the paper's field experiment is a genuine production A/B-style (geographic DID) deployment, evaluated on predicted outcomes at recommendation, realized outcomes at two weeks, and post-engagement (messaging) outcomes at two weeks. This is the only paper in the batch with a live field experiment specifically evaluating a congestion-management mechanism.

**Reproducibility.** Predictive model feature sets and specifications are proprietary and not disclosed ("the detailed feature set and model specifications are proprietary"). Simulation parameters (capacity sweeps, market sizes, calibration dates) are specified precisely. The full ECDA algorithm (Theorem 2's greedy-equivalent form) is given explicitly, making the mechanism itself reproducible even though the underlying prediction models are not.

**Overall.** The paper is candid about its own weakest link: the DID's two-cluster inference problem, which it addresses by (a) trimming the top 0.1% of receiver-days and showing the qualitative conclusion is robust at 0.5% and 1% thresholds too, and (b) leaning on the pre-registered, event-study-supported design rather than claiming formal statistical certainty.

## 3. Industry Contribution

**Deployability.** High — this is a paper about production deployment, not just a theoretical proposal. The greedy-equivalence theorems (dating-rate-sorted DA/ECDA reduce to a single sort plus a linear scan with constant-time capacity updates) are specifically what made large-scale deployment on CoupLink feasible; the authors state plainly that without these results, DA and ECDA "may not be implementable at the platform scale."

**Problems solved.** Converts an existing production prediction stack (four independently trained binary classifiers for login/like/relike behavior) into a congestion-aware recommendation mechanism without retraining any of the underlying models — the integration happens entirely at the mechanism-design layer, on top of predictions treated as fixed inputs.

**Engineering cost.** Relatively low incremental cost given the greedy-equivalence results: the deployed mechanism is "a single sorting step (and a linear scan thereafter)," and the field experiment's capacity setting (1.5 expected dates/day) was chosen to be "loose" — binding only for a small subset of exceptionally high-relike-rate receivers — explicitly to keep operational risk low. The paper reports this was treated as a low-risk business change precisely because most receivers experienced no reduction in recommendation opportunities.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

**Claimed novelty.** First integration of multi-dimensional behavioral predictions (activity/login, like, and reciprocation/relike, not preference/like alone) into a matching-theoretic recommendation mechanism; first framing of receiver-side capacity as an exposure quota tied to expected likes or dates rather than an exogenous headcount; a novel congestion-adjusted outcome metric (effective dates); and validation via both calibrated simulation on real production predictions and a large-scale live field experiment — a combination the authors state prior work has not delivered together.

**Prior work.** Chen, Hsieh, and Lin (2023), "Reducing Recommendation Inequality via Two-Sided Matching: A Field Experiment of Online Dating," International Economic Review — combines regression-based prediction with a transferable-utility matching framework (building on Choo and Siow, 2006, and Galichon and Salanié, 2022) for online-dating equity; this is the same Chen et al. (2023) independently cited by Paper 2 in this batch (Calsamiglia et al.) for the identical congestion-from-preference-ranking finding, reinforcing cross-paper agreement on the phenomenon. Su, Otani (or "Su et al.," 2022) — cited as ranking pairs by social-welfare criteria; this is Paper 1 of this batch (Su, Bayoumi, and Joachims, WWW 2022). Rios et al. (2023) — explicitly flagged as "a notable exception" that, like this paper, combines login probabilities and history-dependent effects within an assortment-optimization framework. Tomita, Yokoyama, and colleagues (2022, 2024) — matching-based recommender evaluation and Nash-social-welfare fairness guarantees for two-sided recommendation. Arnosti, Johari, and Kanoria (2021) and Manshadi and colleagues (2022, 2023) — congestion management and capacity adjustment in matching markets, the theoretical lineage this paper's exposure-quota redefinition of "capacity" builds on. Ashlagi, Aouad, and Saban (2023), Shi (2022, 2023) — assortment/priority design in two-sided environments, adjacent to Paper 4 of this batch (Ashlagi, Krishnaswamy, Makhijani, Saban, and Shiragur). Kanoria and Saban (2021) — design features that restrict search or information in two-sided platforms. Palomares et al. (2021) — cited as the survey paper for reciprocal recommender systems, noting the sub-field's relative recency and thinness of prior comparative analysis.

## Project Relevance

This paper is the batch's clearest example of the exact seam the project must build across — **Q5** (where uplift/incremental effects sit inside the ranking model) is only partially addressed (the field experiment is incremental at the *mechanism* level, not the individual-recommendation level), but the paper directly answers **Q7** (congestion for a shared limited resource, two-sided market) with a production-validated mechanism, and it is directly relevant to **Q4** in that it shows how *four separately trained* behavioral prediction heads (login×2, like, relike) can be composed into a single dating-rate score and then consumed by a downstream matching mechanism — a fixed-fusion pattern, not a learned or unified head, but a concrete precedent for the "combine short-term event heads" question. It speaks to **Q6** (offline and online evaluation under two-sided interference) with an unusually strong real answer: a geographic DID design explicitly built to minimize cross-user interference, plus the paper's own transparent acknowledgment of its inference limits with only two clusters — directly useful for the project's evaluation-plan deliverable. It does **not** address **Q1, Q2, Q3, or Q8**: there is no retention or revenue training objective, no delayed-label handling beyond a 2-week post-engagement window, and no migration path from a CTR-style model to a unified value model (the four component predictors here remain separate, fused only at the mechanism layer, not the model layer).

Horizon verdict: 2-week realized-outcome and post-engagement window (daily recommendation refresh cycle) — short-horizon; no retention (7–30 day) or subscription-revenue objective is addressed.
Lever verdict: a recommendation matrix combining within-slate ranking (rank-order list by dating rate) with a cross-proposer exposure quota per receiver (capacity defined on expected likes/dates, not headcount) — a ranking-only platform can implement the rank-order-list half directly, but the exposure-quota half requires cross-session inventory/pacing state beyond a single ranking call, unless approximated by feeding real-time congestion signals back into the ranking model itself as a feature.
