## 1. Summary
Title: Two-Stage Constrained Actor-Critic for Short Video Recommendation (TSCAC)
Authors: Qingpeng Cai, Zhenghai Xue, Chi Zhang, Wanqi Xue, Shuchang Liu, Ruohan Zhan, et al. (Kuaishou Technology)
Venue/Year: WWW '23, May 1–5, 2023, Austin, TX, USA

Abstract/key contributions: Short-video platforms must jointly optimize a dense main signal (WatchTime, correlated with DAU) and multiple sparse auxiliary interaction signals (Like, Follow, Comment, Share, Collect, Hate). Naively summing these into one joint reward for a single critic causes the dense WatchTime signal to dominate and drown out sparse signals; standard constrained-RL methods (e.g., RCPO with a single Lagrangian multiplier) also fail because (a) a single value/critic model cannot accommodate response-specific discount factors and differing observation frequencies, and (b) multiple simultaneous constraints create a large, expensive-to-grid-search multi-dimensional Lagrangian-multiplier space. The paper formulates short-video recommendation as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) and proposes a Two-Stage Constrained Actor-Critic (TSCAC): Stage One trains a separate critic and policy actor per response (multi-critic policy estimation, each with its own discount factor gamma_i), avoiding cross-response interference. Stage Two trains the main-response policy to maximize its own advantage while being "softly" regularized (via a closed-form KL-divergence-derived optimal-policy solution) to stay close to the Stage-One auxiliary policies, rather than tuning Lagrangian multipliers directly.

Methodology: CMDP (S,A,P,R,C,rho_0,Gamma) with vector-valued reward r(s,a)=(r_1,...,r_m) and per-response discount vector Gamma=(gamma_1,...,gamma_m). Stage One: for each auxiliary response i, train critic V_phi_i via TD/Bellman minimization and actor pi_theta_i via policy-gradient advantage maximization, independently. Stage Two: for the main response, train critic V_phi_1 via standard TD learning; the actor's constrained optimization problem max E[A_1] s.t. D_KL(pi||pi_theta_i) <= epsilon_i for i=2..m has a closed-form Lagrangian solution pi*(a|s) proportional to the product of the auxiliary policies raised to normalized Lagrangian-multiplier powers, times exp(A_1/sum(lambda)); the main policy is then updated by minimizing its KL divergence from this closed-form pi*. In production, all lambda_i are set equal for practicality. Extensions are given for offline (importance-sampling bias correction with a first-order approximation) and deterministic/continuous-action policies (using a Gaussian-RBF-style action-proximity scoring function h(a1,a2) in place of the discrete-action KL term).

Main results: On the public KuaiRand dataset (offline, NCIS off-policy evaluation), TSCAC achieved the best WatchTime score (13.14, +2.23% over Behavior Cloning) while also ranking best on Click, Like, and Comment and lowest (best) on Hate among constrained-RL methods — beating RCPO (best WatchTime alternative, but worst Hate) and Pareto (best Hate, but worst WatchTime, -7.4%). On TripAdvisor (offline), TSCAC achieved the top overall rating (3.99) and led on 5 of 7 auxiliary aspect scores. In a live one-week A/B test on an industrial short-video platform, TSCAC improved WatchTime by +0.379% and Share/Download by +3.376%/+1.733% over the production LTR baseline, outperforming RCPO and an Interaction-AC ablation.

## 2. Experiment Critique
Design: Two offline datasets (public KuaiRand — 26,858 users, 10.2M items, 68.1M samples, with click/like/comment/hate sparsity ratios of 37.70%/1.61%/0.24%/0.048%; and TripAdvisor — 20,277 customers, 150 hotels, 257,932 reviews with 8 rating aspects) plus a live A/B test on an industrial short-video platform. Baselines include BC, Wide&Deep, DeepFM (weighted-sum supervised baselines), RCPO and an author-improved RCPO-Multi-Critic, and Pareto (multi-objective RL).

Statistical validity: The paper reports relative percentage gaps vs. BC per metric with best-baseline flags (asterisks) but does not report explicit significance tests (e.g., p-values) for the offline KuaiRand/TripAdvisor tables in the retrieved excerpts; the authors do explicitly state that "0.1% improvement of WatchTime and 1% improvement of interactions are statistically significant" on the live short-video platform, treating online results as significant by platform-specific convention.

Online experiments: A genuine one-week live production A/B test with random user-bucket assignment (one bucket per algorithm: LTR baseline, RCPO, Interaction-AC ablation, TSCAC), evaluated on WatchTime, Share, Download, Comment — a real, well-specified online evaluation.

Reproducibility: Hyperparameter and MDP specification details (state/action/reward definitions, discount factor 0.99, Lagrangian multiplier ablation range [1e-1..1e-5]) are given for both offline datasets; the live experiment's production system, feature set, and exact traffic-splitting mechanics are less reproducible outside the host platform.

Overall: The ablation on the Lagrangian multiplier lambda (Figure 3) is a genuine strength — it shows both directions of failure (too weak a constraint hurts auxiliary metrics; too strong a constraint hurts the main WatchTime metric), giving a credible picture of the method's sensitivity rather than only showing the best operating point.

## 3. Industry Contribution
Deployability: Fully launched in a popular short-video production system (Kuaishou), replacing/augmenting the LTR default ranking model at the candidate-ranking stage; the deterministic-policy extension (continuous action = an embedding of predicted user preference over topics, matched to candidates via dot product) matches standard low-latency industrial retrieval/ranking patterns already in use.

Problems solved: Solves a concrete, previously-unsolved production pain point — how to hold multiple sparse auxiliary interaction constraints (Like/Follow/Comment/Share) roughly stable while maximizing a dominant, dense main objective (WatchTime), without the compute-prohibitive multi-dimensional Lagrangian-multiplier grid search that standard constrained RL would require.

Engineering cost: Requires training m separate actor-critic pairs (one main + one per auxiliary response) rather than a single joint model — roughly m times the actor-critic training surface — though only the final Stage-Two main policy needs to be served online; the auxiliary policies are training-time artifacts used to define the soft KL regularization, not separately executed production heads in the deterministic-action version.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work
Claimed novelty: (1) Formalizing short-video multi-response optimization as a CMDP with per-response discount factors and multiple simultaneous constraints (rather than one constraint, as in prior work); (2) multi-critic policy estimation to avoid a dense signal drowning out sparse ones in a single joint value model; (3) a two-stage soft-regularization scheme with a closed-form optimal-policy solution, replacing expensive multi-dimensional Lagrangian-multiplier search with pre-trained per-response policies that the main policy is regularized toward.

Prior work cited (from Query 2 part 3): Tessler, Mankowitz, and Mannor (2018), "Reward constrained policy optimization" (RCPO) — the core single-constraint constrained-RL baseline this paper generalizes to multiple constraints; Chen, Du, Xia, and Wang (2021), "Reinforcement Recommendation with User Multi-aspect Preference" — source of the TripAdvisor benchmark and the Pareto-optimization comparison point; Sutton and Barto (2018), "Reinforcement learning: An introduction" — CMDP formalism; Dulac-Arnold et al. (2015), "Deep reinforcement learning in large discrete action spaces" — production dot-product action-to-candidate matching function; Precup (2000) / Precup, Sutton, Dasgupta (2001) — importance-sampling bias correction for the offline extension; Williams (1992), "Simple statistical gradient-following algorithms" — stochastic policy-gradient foundation; Zou, Xia, Ding, Song, Liu, Yin (2019), "Reinforcement learning to optimize long-term user engagement in recommender systems" — source of the NCIS offline evaluation protocol.

## 7. Reference Card
1. **Title, authors/company, venue, year, URL:** "Two-Stage Constrained Actor-Critic for Short Video Recommendation," Qingpeng Cai, Zhenghai Xue, Chi Zhang, Wanqi Xue, Shuchang Liu, Ruohan Zhan, et al. (Kuaishou Technology), WWW '23, Austin, TX, USA. URL: https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583259. `nlm:7b46f4f0-1894-4ce7-a0e3-603669e259f8`.
2. **Source type:** Academic (industry-affiliated; deployed and A/B-tested in Kuaishou production).
3. **Direction:** D2.
4. **Problem setting:** Short-video (and, secondarily, hotel-review) recommendation where the system must maximize one dense main engagement signal (WatchTime / overall rating) subject to not sacrificing several sparser auxiliary interaction signals (Like/Follow/Share/Comment/Hate, or multi-aspect ratings) that arrive at different frequencies and warrant different discount factors.
5. **Objective and label definition:** Constrained-MDP objective — maximize expected discounted cumulative main-response reward subject to lower-bound constraints on auxiliary-response expected values (max E[V_1] s.t. E[V_i]>=C_i). Labels: main = WatchTime (continuous, dense, observed every video view) or hotel overall rating; auxiliary = binary interaction indicators (Like/Follow/Comment/Collect/Share/Hate) or multi-aspect hotel scores, observed sparsely (e.g., Like 1.61%, Comment 0.24%, Hate 0.048% of samples in KuaiRand). Horizon: infinite-horizon discounted MDP, gamma=0.99, organized by user session (app open to leave). Delay/censoring handling: Not specified in source.
6. **Prediction or incrementality:** Prediction only — the paper's own framing states "Value-based approaches estimate user satisfaction of being recommended an item... Policy-based methods directly learn the policy... and optimize it in the direction of increasing user satisfaction," and "the goal of the recommender system is to optimize cumulative reward of the main response... with the constraint of not sacrificing others much." This is standard RL policy-value optimization against observed/simulated reward; no counterfactual, treatment-vs-control, or incremental-effect estimation is performed anywhere in the method.
7. **Model architecture:** Per-response actor pi_theta_i and critic V_phi_i (Stage One, m-1 auxiliary pairs) plus a main actor pi_theta_1 and critic V_phi_1 (Stage Two) whose update uses a closed-form KL-regularized solution derived via Lagrangian duality; for continuous/deterministic action spaces (the production setting), a Gaussian-policy / deterministic-actor variant is used with an action-proximity scoring function h(a1,a2) replacing the discrete-action KL term.
8. **Credit assignment:** This paper's credit-assignment mechanism operates across objectives, not across time/items — it decomposes and separately evaluates which response (WatchTime vs. Like vs. Follow, etc.) drives value, via one critic per response, rather than decomposing a single delayed outcome across the sequence of items/steps that preceded it. Standard per-step TD credit assignment (r_i(s,a) + gamma_i·V_i(s') - V_i(s)) is used within each response's own critic; there is no cross-session, delayed-outcome-to-single-item decomposition of the kind found in the other two Kuaishou papers in this batch (GFN4Retention's flow-based retention attribution, or Future Impact Decomposition's item-wise future-value split within a slate). Each recommended item is a single action a_t in the offline KuaiRand MDP (one video ID, not an explicit slate), so credit assignment here is standard one-step-back TD learning per response, applied independently per objective.
9. **Training data and counterfactual handling:** Offline — trajectories built by concatenating each user's logged interaction history into episodes (top-150-viewed videos per user for KuaiRand; length-filtered reviews for TripAdvisor); an importance-sampling bias-correction (first-order approximation of the exact trajectory-based ratio) is applied when adapting the online algorithm to a fixed offline dataset, addressing off-policy distribution mismatch — this is a policy-gradient bias correction, not a causal/counterfactual-outcome estimation.
10. **Offline and online evaluation:** Offline — Normalised Capped Importance Sampling (NCIS), a standard RL off-policy evaluation protocol (Zou et al. 2019), on KuaiRand and TripAdvisor; also Discounted Cumulative Gain (DCG) reported in an appendix. Online — one-week live A/B test on an industrial short-video candidate-ranking system, randomly split traffic buckets (baseline LTR, RCPO, Interaction-AC, TSCAC), evaluated on WatchTime, Share, Download, Comment percentage lifts vs. the LTR baseline.
11. **Reported gains:** Offline (KuaiRand, NCIS score vs. BC): TSCAC WatchTime 13.14 (+2.23%), Click 0.5570 (+4.35%), Like 1.462e-2 (+18.80%), Comment 3.728e-3 (+15.60%), Hate 1.870e-4 (-18.83%, i.e., improved/lower) — best or tied-best on 4 of 5 metrics vs. RCPO, RCPO-Multi-Critic, and Pareto. Offline (TripAdvisor): overall rating 3.99 (tied with RCPO-Multi-Critic), best on 5 of 7 auxiliary aspects. Online (industrial platform, vs. LTR baseline): TSCAC WatchTime +0.379%, Share +3.376%, Download +1.733%, Comment -0.619% (an improvement over RCPO's -1.313% comment decline).
12. **Applicability to a two-sided dating recommender:** Not addressed directly (single-sided video/hotel-review setting, no reciprocity/congestion/fairness treatment). The two-stage soft-KL-regularization mechanism for balancing one dominant objective against several auxiliary constraints is directly analogous to the project's own tension between retention, revenue, and match quality, and offers a concrete alternative to fixed-weight linear blending.
13. **Unverified claims:** The claim that setting all Lagrangian multipliers lambda_i equal is "more practical for the production system" is asserted as a practicality trade-off without a systematic comparison against per-response-tuned lambda values (the paper acknowledges "the performance of TSCAC would be better if we fine-tune it with different Lagrangian multiplier value" but does not quantify the gap). The universal drop in Comment across all RL methods online is attributed to "a natural trade-off between WatchTime and Comment" without a controlled analysis isolating that causal mechanism from other confounds.

## Project Relevance
This paper speaks primarily to **Q4** (combining short-term/dense and long-term/sparse objective heads — here, one dense main signal and several sparse auxiliary signals via per-response critics) and to the project's stated tension between retention, revenue, and match quality: TSCAC's two-stage soft-KL-regularization pattern is a directly applicable alternative to a fixed post-hoc blend (the project's current CTR/CVR-plus-uplift blend) for the survey's staged-migration question (**Q8**). It is weak on **Q2** (delayed-outcome-to-item credit assignment) — its credit assignment is across-objective (per-response critics), not across-time/within-slate, so it is complementary to, not a replacement for, GFN4Retention or Future Impact Decomposition's mechanisms in this batch. It does not address **causal incrementality (Q5)** or **two-sided/reciprocal-market dynamics (Q7)** — entirely single-sided. Its multi-critic design (separate value estimation per objective, avoiding a dense signal drowning out a sparse one) is a concrete, evidence-backed answer to a problem the project will face directly given its own low base rates for matches/conversations relative to more frequent engagement signals.
