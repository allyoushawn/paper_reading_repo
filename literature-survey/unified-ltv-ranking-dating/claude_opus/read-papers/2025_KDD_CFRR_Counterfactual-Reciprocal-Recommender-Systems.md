## 1. Summary

Kazuki Kawamura, Takuma Udagawa, Kei Tateno (Sony Group Corporation, Japan), "Counterfactual Reciprocal Recommender Systems for User-to-User Matching," TSMO '25 (KDD 2025 Workshop on Two-Sided Marketplace Optimization: Search, Discovery, Matching, Pricing & Growth), Toronto, Canada, August 4, 2025.

**Abstract (paraphrased):** Reciprocal recommender systems (RRS) in dating, gaming, and talent platforms require mutual acceptance for a match. Logged data over-represents popular profiles because of past exposure policies, creating a feedback loop that skews learning and fairness. The paper introduces Counterfactual Reciprocal Recommender Systems (CFRR), a causal framework using inverse-propensity-scored, self-normalized objectives. Experiments show CFRR improves NDCG@10 by up to 3.5% (e.g., 0.459→0.475 on DBLP), increases long-tail user coverage by up to 51% (0.504→0.763 on Synthetic), and reduces Gini exposure inequality by up to 24% (0.708→0.535 on Synthetic).

**Key contributions:** (1) CFRR, the first unified causal framework for correcting exposure bias specifically in bilateral (user-to-user) matching, as distinct from item recommendation — the paper explicitly identifies three ways user-user matching differs from item-based IPS: bilateral feedback (a match needs mutual acceptance), pair-level propensity (the display probability θ(u,v) depends jointly on both users), and downstream application complexity (scores feed stable-matching or fairness-constrained allocation algorithms). (2) A Self-Normalized IPS (SNIPS) objective adapted to pair-level propensities, preventing a few extreme-weight rarely-shown pairs from dominating training. (3) Variance-reduction extensions: weight truncation/clipping and a doubly-robust (DR) augmentation adapted to the reciprocal setting, giving consistency if either the propensity model or the outcome model is correctly specified. (4) A joint propensity-learning routine that alternately updates the matching-score model Θ and the propensity model β as the logging policy evolves.

**Methodology:** For a pair (u,v), the platform's display decision O(u,v)∈{0,1} has propensity θ(u,v)=P[O(u,v)=1|u,v], modeled parametrically as σ(g(φ(u),ψ(v);β)) and fit by maximum likelihood on observed exposure logs. The target is the true population risk L(Θ)=E_(u,v)~Unif(P)[ℓ(R(u,v),s(u,v;Θ))] over the full pair space P=U×V. IPS reweights each observed (uᵢ,vᵢ,rᵢ) by 1/θ(uᵢ,vᵢ) to give a consistent estimator of L(Θ); SNIPS additionally normalizes by the sum of weights across the batch/dataset to stabilize training under extreme low-propensity weights. A DR variant further subtracts and adds back a pre-trained outcome model r̂(u,v) for a baseline-corrected, doubly-robust objective. Weight truncation caps raw weights at a tunable ceiling c to bound variance at the cost of possible bias. Joint propensity learning periodically freezes Θ and re-estimates β via maximum likelihood as the platform's exposure policy evolves.

**Main results:** Evaluated on Synthetic (5K users, 16D latent factors, known ground truth, tunable exposure bias, ~50K logged pairs), DBLP-CoAuthor (up to 10K authors, co-authorship as reciprocal match), and Epinions-Trust (up to 5K users, mutual trust as reciprocal match), against LFRR, CausE, IPW-MF, DICE, and StableDR baselines. CFRR-SNIPS achieves the best NDCG@10 on all three datasets (Synthetic: 0.307 vs. 0.299 LFRR, +2.7%; DBLP: 0.475 vs. 0.459 LFRR, +3.5%; Epinions: 0.472 vs. LFRR, +0.9%), all statistically significant (p<0.05, Bonferroni-corrected paired t-tests over 10 seeds). Fairness gains are larger: Coverage@10 +51% and Gini-Exposure −24% on Synthetic (both statistically significant); similar but more modest gains on DBLP/Epinions. Ablations show SNIPS materially outperforms vanilla IPS on fairness metrics (Coverage@10 +37% relative), and the DR variant is meaningfully more robust than plain SNIPS when the propensity model is deliberately misspecified (AUC degraded from >0.9 to ≈0.7).

## 2. Experiment Critique

- **Design:** Three datasets with genuinely distinct exposure-bias mechanisms (controlled synthetic ground truth; naturally-arising academic-collaboration popularity bias; naturally-arising social-trust "influencer" bias) is a reasonable design for testing generalization of the debiasing mechanism, and the synthetic dataset with known ground-truth R(u,v) is specifically useful for isolating bias-correction accuracy (the paper's stated purpose for including it). Real-world datasets (DBLP, Epinions) use random negative sampling for non-linked pairs, which the authors themselves flag as an imperfect proxy for true non-interaction.
- **Statistical validity:** 10 random seeds per configuration, 20 epochs, paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, and reported effect sizes — a genuinely rigorous statistical protocol relative to the rest of this batch.
- **Online experiments:** None. All evaluation is offline, on static logged/synthetic datasets; no live A/B test on an actual reciprocal-matching platform (dating, gaming, or talent) is reported, despite dating platforms being named as the headline motivating example in the abstract and introduction.
- **Reproducibility:** DBLP and Epinions are public SNAP datasets (https://snap.stanford.edu/data/com-DBLP.html, https://snap.stanford.edu/data/soc-Epinions1.html); synthetic data generation is described at a high level with full details deferred to supplementary material; code availability is stated as forthcoming ("will be made available upon publication") rather than currently public.
- **Overall:** Methodologically careful within an entirely offline, link-prediction-style evaluation; the absence of any live deployment evidence on an actual reciprocal marketplace is a real gap the authors do not fully close, despite Sony's industry affiliation.

## 3. Industry Contribution

- **Deployability:** Moderate-to-high — IPS/SNIPS/DR reweighting is a well-understood, computationally cheap correction (just a weighted loss), and the joint propensity-learning loop (Algorithm 1) is designed explicitly with production reality in mind: it addresses the case where the logging policy has no explicit exploration and is continuously evolving, which is the typical state of a live ranking system rather than a controlled experiment.
- **Problems solved:** The paper explicitly addresses trillions-of-pairs positivity infeasibility (θ(u,v)>0 cannot hold for every conceivable pair at marketplace scale) by proposing to restrict debiasing to an economically/geographically viable candidate subset generated by retrieval, transforming an intractable theoretical requirement into a manageable engineering constraint — directly relevant to how a dating app's candidate-generation stage would need to interact with this kind of correction.
- **Engineering cost:** Requires (a) a propensity model trained on exposure logs, (b) the compatibility-scoring model itself (any RRS scorer, e.g., LFRR-style latent factors), (c) for the DR variant, a separately pre-trained outcome model r̂(u,v), and (d) if using joint propensity learning, an alternating-optimization training loop. The authors report this adds only 5–10% training-time overhead for SNIPS and ~20% for the DR variant over a baseline RRS model — a modest, quantified cost. Downstream, the debiased scores are designed to feed a stable-matching algorithm (Gale-Shapley) or a maximum-weight bipartite matching solver under capacity constraints, which is directly the kind of allocation step a production dating-recommender pipeline would run after scoring.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

**Claimed novelty:** The first unified causal (IPS/SNIPS/DR) framework purpose-built for pair-level exposure bias in reciprocal (bilateral) recommendation, as distinct from prior causal-debiasing work built for single-sided item recommendation; the adaptation of self-normalization and doubly-robust augmentation to pair-level propensities; the joint propensity-learning routine for an evolving logging policy; and the explicit connection of debiased scores to downstream stable-matching/fairness-constrained allocation.

**Prior work most heavily built on:**
- Swaminathan and Joachims, "Counterfactual Risk Minimization: Learning from Logged Bandit Feedback," 2015 — the foundational SNIPS formulation CFRR adapts to pair-level propensities.
- Schnabel, Swaminathan, Singh, Chandak, and Joachims, "Recommendations as Treatments: Debiasing Learning and Evaluation," 2016 — the "recommendations as interventions"/IPS framing this paper directly extends to reciprocal matching.
- Gale and Shapley, "College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage," 1962 — the stable-matching algorithm named as CFRR's primary downstream application.
- Neve and Palomares, "Latent Factor Models and Aggregation Operators for Collaborative Filtering in Reciprocal Recommender Systems," 2019 — source of the LFRR baseline.
- Li, Zheng, and Wu, "StableDR: Stabilized Doubly Robust Learning for Recommendation on Data Missing Not at Random," 2022 — basis for the StableDR baseline CFRR is compared against.
- Zheng, Gao, Li, He, Li, and Jin, "Disentangling User Interest and Conformity for Recommendation with Causal Embedding," 2021 — basis for the DICE baseline.
- Biega, Gummadi, and Weikum, "Equity of Attention: Amortizing Individual Fairness in Rankings," 2018 — the Gini-Exposure fairness metric CFRR's evaluation is built around.

## Project Relevance

This is the survey's single most on-target paper for combining uplift/causal reasoning with reciprocal (user-to-user) recommendation — precisely the intersection the project sits at — and it directly and centrally answers **Q2** (credit assignment: exact pair-level attribution mechanism, see field 8 above) and **Q7** (two-sided/reciprocal market: reciprocity is the paper's entire subject; congestion is addressed via the paper's explicit connection to downstream stable-matching and maximum-weight-matching allocation under capacity constraints; fairness across sides is addressed via Coverage@10 and Gini-Exposure). It partially informs **Q5** (uplift/incrementality) in a way that must be stated precisely rather than assumed: CFRR uses causal/counterfactual reweighting machinery, but to correct *selection bias in a prediction target* (mutual acceptance), not to estimate the *incremental effect of exposure* on retention or revenue — see field 6 above. Readers should not conflate "this paper uses IPS" with "this paper does uplift modeling for the project's cascade objective"; it does not.

Does not address **Q1** (no retention/revenue training objective at all — the label is match/acceptance only), **Q3** (no time horizon, delay, or censoring treatment whatsoever), **Q4** (no fusion of short-term and long-term heads — there is only one prediction target), **Q6** (offline evaluation only, no online design under two-sided interference is discussed), or **Q8** (no migration path from a CTR/CVR + uplift blend is discussed). The paper's real contribution to the project is as a building block — a validated method for correcting exposure/selection bias inside a reciprocal ranking model — that would need to be combined with a retention/revenue objective and delay-handling machinery from elsewhere in the survey (this batch's papers 1–3 are natural complements).
