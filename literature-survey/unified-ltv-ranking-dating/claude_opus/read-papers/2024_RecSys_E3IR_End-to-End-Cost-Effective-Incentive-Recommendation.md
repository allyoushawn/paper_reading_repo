## 1. Summary

Zexu Sun, Hao Yang, Dugang Liu, Yunpeng Weng, Xing Tang, Xiuqiang He (Renmin University of China; Tencent), "End-to-End Cost-Effective Incentive Recommendation under Budget Constraint with Uplift Modeling," RecSys 2024 (18th ACM Conference on Recommender Systems), pp. 560–569 (submitted as a preprint 24 August 2024). The paper targets budget-constrained incentive recommendation — selecting one incentive level per customer, under a global budget cap, formalized as a Multi-Choice Knapsack Problem (MCKP) — and argues that the standard two-stage "predict uplift, then optimize allocation" pipeline has two failure modes: (1) causal-inference uplift predictors ignore marketing domain knowledge, namely that a customer's expected response should be monotonic and smooth as incentive level increases, so unconstrained models produce erratic, non-monotonic curves that lead to suboptimal allocation; and (2) an "optimality gap" opens because the prediction stage is trained for pointwise accuracy while the downstream knapsack allocation cares about decision quality, and these two objectives diverge. The proposed **E³IR** framework has two interconnected modules trained end-to-end: an uplift-prediction module that reparameterizes outputs as cumulative non-negative incremental effects between adjacent treatment levels (guaranteeing monotonicity) plus a Lipschitz-regularization smoothness term, and a differentiable-allocation module that embeds the MCKP as a differentiable layer (following the OptNet/ComboptNet line of differentiable combinatorial optimization) so that allocation-loss gradients flow back and directly reshape the upstream uplift predictions toward better downstream decisions, not just better pointwise accuracy. Its own words are explicit that the estimand is a treatment effect: "uplift modeling, which aims to predict the incremental impact of incentives on customer behavior rather than just the likelihood of an outcome." Evaluated on the public Hillstrom email-marketing dataset (binary treatment) and a production dataset from a major Chinese short-video platform with over 8 million users (multi-treatment), against two-stage predict-then-optimize baselines and the DRP/DRM state-of-the-art causal resource-allocation model; E³IR improved the Qini coefficient on Hillstrom-Men from 0.0683 (best baseline) to 0.0717, and on the production dataset achieved MT-AUCC 0.4639 and Expected Outcome Metric 44.5087, substantially outperforming baselines; ablations show removing the differentiable-allocation module causes the largest single performance drop, confirming the end-to-end coupling — not just the monotonic/smooth constraints — is doing the most work.

## Project Relevance

E³IR is the batch's clearest architectural analogue to what the project's target system needs to do structurally, even though its domain (budget-constrained incentive allocation) differs from ranking: it demonstrates a working, empirically-validated pattern for jointly training a causal/incremental estimate against a downstream decision objective instead of blending a separately-trained prediction and a separately-trained uplift model after the fact — directly informative for **Q8** (migration paths from a CTR/CVR + uplift blend to a unified objective). Its uplift-prediction module is genuine incrementality (see field 6), reinforcing that end-to-end predict-and-decide coupling is achievable in production-scale industrial settings (the production dataset has 8M+ users, though the evaluation itself is offline).

It does not inform **Q1** (no retention/revenue training objective — the label is a per-incentive response/cost, not retention or revenue), **Q2** (credit assignment is single-user, single-decision; see field 8 — no item/impression/slate mapping exists), **Q3** (no time horizon, delay, or censoring treatment anywhere), **Q4** (a single uplift-prediction module, no short-term/long-term head fusion), **Q6** (offline evaluation only; no online design under interference is discussed), or **Q7** (a one-sided platform-to-customer incentive-allocation setting; no reciprocity, congestion, or two-sided fairness).

Counterexample verdict: NO — E³IR is a genuine, architecturally sophisticated uplift-and-allocation framework, but it is not a ranking model (the decision is a discrete incentive choice per user under a knapsack constraint, not a ranked list of candidate profiles) and its objective has no defined time horizon or delay/censoring handling, so it fails both the "ranking model" and "long-horizon objective" legs of the provisional conclusion despite clearly satisfying the "incrementality" leg.
