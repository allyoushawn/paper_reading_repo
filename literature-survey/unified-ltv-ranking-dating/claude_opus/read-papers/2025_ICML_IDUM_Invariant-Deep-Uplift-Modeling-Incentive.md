## 1. Summary

Zexu Sun, Qiyu Han, Hao Yang, Anpeng Wu, Minqin Zhu, Dugang Liu, Chen Ma, Yunpeng Weng, Xing Tang, Xiuqiang He, "Invariant Deep Uplift Modeling for Incentive Assignment in Online Marketing via Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency," ICML 2025 (Spotlight Poster; OpenReview). The paper targets a generalization failure in existing uplift models for incentive assignment (discounts, coupons): they are trained and evaluated under an in-distribution (ID) assumption, but real platforms see continuous feature drift by time, geography, and season, causing out-of-distribution (OOD) uplift-prediction failures and inappropriate incentive assignment. The proposed method, **IDUM (Invariant Deep Uplift Modeling)**, combines three components: (1) Invariant Property Learning (IPL), which uses Pearl's Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency (PNS) to disentangle domain-invariant causal features into necessary and sufficient factors, with a PNS-risk upper bound derived via H-divergence and PAC-learning to bound target-environment risk by source-environment risk; (2) a Gumbel-Softmax-based feature-selection mask to reduce the computational cost of searching high-dimensional invariant feature sets; (3) a CFRNet-style Integral Probability Metric (IPM) balancing-discrepancy term to correct treatment/control selection bias. The label is the standard CATE-style uplift object — "the objective of uplift modeling is to evaluate the effect of treatment T on the response Y... calculating the difference between treatment and control responses," with CATE used as the estimable proxy for the unobservable individual treatment effect via the potential-outcome framework. Evaluated on the Lazada (Southeast Asian e-commerce, real voucher-distribution logs with selection-biased training data and an RCT-based OOD test subset) and a Chinese short-video-platform Production dataset (one-week randomized video-clarity experiment, response = discretized weekly play count, with two time points for OOD testing), against S-Learner, T-Learner, TARNet, CFRNet, DragonNet, EUEN, UniTE, and TEED, using AUUC/QINI/Kendall metrics. IDUM consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest margin on OOD test data; an ablation shows the invariant-property-learning (PNS) component contributes the largest single drop in OOD performance when removed.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

Abbreviated per priority-3 depth rule. Builds most heavily on: Judea Pearl's Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency framework (Pearl, *Causality*, 2009; "Probabilities of Causation," 2022) — the paper's core causal-identification tool; Shalit, Johansson, and Sontag, "Estimating Individual Treatment Effect: Generalization Bounds and Algorithms" (TARNet/CFRNet), ICML 2017 — source of the representation-balancing baseline and IPM mechanism IDUM extends; Shi, Blei, and Veitch, "Adapting Neural Networks for the Estimation of Treatment Effects" (Dragonnet), NeurIPS 2019 — baseline; Künzel, Sekhon, Bickel, and Yu, "Metalearners for Estimating Heterogeneous Treatment Effects," PNAS 2019 — S-/T-Learner baselines; Arjovsky, Bottou, Gulrajani, and Lopez-Paz, "Invariant Risk Minimization," 2019 — the OOD-generalization tradition IDUM's invariant-learning component sits in; Yang et al., "Invariant Learning via Probability of Sufficient and Necessary Causes," NeurIPS 2024 — the closest prior PNS-based invariant-learning work, which IDUM extends into uplift modeling specifically.

| 9 | Training data and counterfactual handling | Potential-outcome framework (Rubin, 2005): observational data D = {(X_i, T_i, Y_i)} from a source environment e, target environment e′ has only feature data (for OOD evaluation). ## Project Relevance

IDUM is a legitimate, well-evidenced uplift/CATE model (see field 6) — the project's *current* system already has a component like this (the standalone uplift model that estimates extra retention/revenue from a like/match/conversation), so IDUM speaks most directly to **making that existing component more robust to distribution drift**, not to the project's target of *unifying* prediction and incrementality into one model. It does not inform **Q1** (no retention/revenue training objective — labels are voucher response and one-week video play count), **Q2** (credit assignment is user-level only; see field 8 — no item/impression/slate mapping exists), **Q3** (no time horizon beyond the Production dataset's fixed one week; no delay/censoring treatment), **Q4** (single uplift head, no short-term/long-term fusion), **Q6** (offline-only evaluation, no online design under interference), **Q7** (one-sided incentive-assignment setting; no two-sided market, congestion, or reciprocity), or **Q8** (no migration path from a blended CTR/CVR + uplift system — IDUM assumes the uplift model already exists in isolation and only asks how to make it generalize better).

Counterexample verdict: NO — IDUM is a genuine uplift/CATE estimator, but it is not a ranking model (credit assignment is a single user-level treatment decision, not an item/impression-level one) and its objective has no long horizon (one week at most, undefined for the other dataset) — it fails the "ranking model" and "long-horizon objective" legs even though the "incrementality" leg is clearly satisfied.
