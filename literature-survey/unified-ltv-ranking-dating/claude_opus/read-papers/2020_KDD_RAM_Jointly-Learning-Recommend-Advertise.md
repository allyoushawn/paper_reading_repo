# Paper Analysis: Jointly Learning to Recommend and Advertise

**Source:** `/Users/fox/Projects/Awesome-Deep-Learning-Papers-for-Search-Recommendation-Advertising/09_Reinforcement_Learning/2020 (Bytedance) (KDD) [RAM] Jointly Learning to Recommend and Advertise.pdf`
**Date analyzed:** 2026-08-17

## 1. Summary

Zhao, Zheng, Yang, Liu, and Tang (Michigan State University / ByteDance) address the problem that recommendation and advertising on a feed platform (evaluated here on TikTok) are typically optimized by separate teams/models, and that naively ranking both together by one hybrid-list score either produces suboptimal advertising revenue or requires an intractable per-candidate Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) bid computation. They propose RAM (Recommend-Ads Mixed display), a two-level reinforcement-learning framework: a first-level cascading DQN (RS) generates a rec-list of k items from the large recommendation item space, using GRU-encoded browsing history to represent both recommendation and advertising histories, and a cascading Q-decomposition (following the Top-K off-policy-correction pattern from prior YouTube work) to avoid the combinatorial C(|A|,k) action space of selecting-and-ordering a k-item list; a second-level DQN (AS) then decides, for the given rec-list, whether to insert an ad, which ad, and at which of the k+1 possible slots, via a novel architecture that jointly evaluates all (ad, location) pairs by splitting the Q-function into a state-value V(s_t) and an advantage function A(s_t, a_t^ad), reducing per-candidate evaluation cost from O(k·|A_t^as|) forward passes to O(|A_t^as|). Two AS action-selection variants are given: RAM-l (a linear combination of the ad's Q-value and its immediate real-time-bidding revenue, weighted by hyperparameter α) and RAM-n (select the top-N ad-location pairs by Q-value, then let the bidding system pick, among those N, the one with maximal immediate revenue). The framework is trained off-policy from historical logs to avoid a bad-user-experience cold start online, using two separate reward signals — dwell time (or income, for e-commerce) driving the RS's long-term-user-experience objective, and a binary continue-vs-leave signal proxying the negative effect of ads on user experience for the AS — with immediate advertising revenue deliberately excluded from the AS's RL reward since the specific ad shown is chosen by advertiser bids, not the platform. Evaluated on 1M sessions of real TikTok short-video data (188K users, 17.8M regular videos, 10.8M ad videos, 55.23% of rec-lists containing an ad) using a simulator trained on the same dataset to predict dwell time, leave/continue, and ad revenue, comparing against W&D, DeepFM, GRU4Rec, and DRQN baselines plus five ablations isolating the contribution of RL vs. supervised learning, RNN encoding, the cascading DQN, and the V/A decomposition.

## 2. Experiment Critique

**Design.** The evaluation uses a *simulated* online environment, not a live A/B test — the simulator is itself trained on the same real TikTok dataset (predicting dwell time, leave/continue, and ad revenue from a given mixed rec-ads list) and is used both to generate reward signals for offline RL training and to score every method head-to-head in the "Overall Performance Comparison" (Section 4.4). This is an important limitation: the reported gains are gains against a learned simulator of user behavior, not against real user behavior, so the ecological validity of the numbers depends entirely on the simulator's fidelity to the actual test distribution — a version of the standard offline-RL evaluation problem, not resolved by any disclosed simulator-accuracy metric in the retrieved text.

**Statistical validity.** Table 2 reports mean ± standard deviation and p-values for every metric/baseline comparison (e.g., R^rs: RAM-l 19.61±0.23 vs. best baseline GRU 18.99±0.18, p<0.000; RAM-n vs. RAM-l, p=0.006), a genuinely rigorous statistical presentation relative to the other three papers in this batch — but applied entirely to simulator-generated outcomes.

**Online experiments.** None are reported. Algorithm 2 ("Online Test of the RAM Framework") describes an online-serving procedure, but all numbers in Section 4 come from the offline/simulated evaluation; unlike the SlateQ, Alibaba, and Airbnb papers in this same batch — all of which report a real online A/B test — no live-traffic result is disclosed anywhere in the retrieved text.

**Reproducibility.** A dataset sample and implementation code are stated to be released via a Dropbox link cited in the paper; the simulator's own architecture and training procedure are described only at a high level ("shares similar architecture to Figure 5"), so full reproducibility of the evaluation environment is not achievable from the paper text alone.

**Overall.** A methodologically careful *offline* study (proper baselines, an ablation ladder isolating each architectural choice, disclosed variance and significance) whose central weakness is that "performance" is measured entirely against a learned simulator rather than real user/advertiser behavior, with no online validation to close that gap.

## 3. Industry Contribution

RAM is explicitly designed around the real constraint that the platform does not choose which specific ad is inserted — that choice belongs to the real-time-bidding (RTB) advertiser auction under generalized-second-price (GSP) — so the AS's role is narrowed to decisions the platform does control: whether to allow an ad, and where to place it (plus, for RAM-l/RAM-n, how ads are weighted or shortlisted among the auction's eligible candidates). The paper explicitly rejects "rank ads and recs together in one hybrid list" approaches as either suboptimal for ad revenue or requiring an intractable VCG bid computation. The cascading-DQN trick for the RS (reusing an established pattern from prior Top-K off-policy-correction work, itself deployed at YouTube) reduces the RS's action-selection cost from O(C(|A|,k)) to O(k·|A|). The V(s)/A(s,a) split for the AS reduces evaluation cost from O(k·|A^as|) to O(|A^as|) forward passes, a concrete serving-time saving for the ad-insertion decision. The off-policy training procedure (pre-train on historical logs before any online launch) is explicitly motivated by avoiding "bad user experience... when new algorithms have not been well learned" during a cold-start online rollout — a direct, stated risk-mitigation choice for production deployment.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

The claimed novelty is (a) the two-level (RS then AS) RL decomposition of the joint rec+ads problem, replacing the "rank ads and recs together in one hybrid list" pattern criticized above, and (b) the novel DQN architecture for AS that simultaneously evaluates "whether, which, where" via a V(s)/A(s,a) decomposition, which the paper argues neither classical DQN architecture (state-only Q, or state-action Q requiring one forward pass per candidate) can do efficiently. Prior work discussed: **Chen et al., "Top-K off-policy correction for a REINFORCE recommender system," WSDM 2019** — the RS's cascading-DQN state/history-encoding pattern extends this line of prior YouTube work directly (also catalogued elsewhere in this survey's read-papers set). **Zheng et al., "DRN: A deep reinforcement learning framework for news recommendation," WWW 2018** — a DQN-based RS baseline compared against as DRQN in Table 2. The authors' own prior line of work (RecSys 2018 page-wise RL, KDD 2018 pairwise deep RL with negative feedback, arXiv 2019 model-based RL for whole-chain recommendations) establishes the RL-for-recommendation program the RS half extends. On the advertising side, the guaranteed-delivery and real-time-bidding literature (Cai et al., WSDM 2017 RL-based RTB; Wu et al., KDD 2018 multi-agent RTB; Jin et al., CIKM 2018 multi-agent RL bidding) is distinguished from RAM's contribution, which jointly optimizes the *insertion decision* (whether/where) rather than the *bid amount*, which the GSP mechanism resolves separately.

## Project Relevance

Speaks directly to **Q5** (where do uplift/incremental effects sit, and what does that change) — negatively: RAM shows a fully predictive, non-causal architecture for jointly optimizing two competing objectives, so it is useful mainly as a **counter-example / cautionary structural template**: everything downstream (the V/A decomposition, the α trade-off, the training procedure) is built on Q-values that are pure engagement/revenue predictions, and the project's own README explicitly flags "retention conditional on exposure is not the effect of the exposure" as a distinction this paper does not draw anywhere. Speaks to **Q7** and the batch's shared "value of a slate ≠ sum of items" theme via the RAM-n mechanism: an ad's position and presence is chosen jointly with the recommendation list (not scored independently), and the ad-insertion decision is explicitly conditioned on the specific rec-list already generated (state s_t includes a_t^rs) — the same "the slate is not additive" logic that motivates papers 2 and 3 in this batch, applied here to a rec/ad trade-off rather than a same-type-item mutual-influence effect.

Most directly useful for the project's **match-quality-vs-revenue trade-off (the "success paradox" tension)** the README names: RAM-l's α parameter is a hand-set, linear knob controlling how much immediate ad revenue is allowed to override the RS's own long-term-value ranking of where to place an ad — a concrete, if simplistic, worked example of who sets the trade-off and how it is parameterized. RAM-n's alternative — select a Q-value-ranked shortlist, then let a *separate mechanism* (here, the RTB auction; for a dating app, potentially a subscription/monetization policy) pick the final revenue-maximizing choice from within that shortlist — is a second, structurally distinct answer to the same question, worth carrying into the executive summary's staged-migration-path deliverable.

**Low relevance to Q1, Q2, Q3.** The RS objective is dwell time / income, not retention or revenue-with-a-horizon; there is no delayed-label, censoring, or multi-day treatment; and credit assignment, while temporal (TD-based), attributes reward within a single session's trajectory, not across a multi-week delayed retention/revenue outcome. Does not address **Q6**'s online-under-two-sided-interference angle (no live experiment is reported) or **Q8** (no discussion of migrating from an existing "CTR model + separate blend" system — RAM is presented as a from-scratch joint design, not a staged migration).
