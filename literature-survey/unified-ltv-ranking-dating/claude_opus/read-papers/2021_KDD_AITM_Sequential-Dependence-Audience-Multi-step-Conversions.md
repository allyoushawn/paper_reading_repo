# Paper Analysis: Modeling the Sequential Dependence among Audience Multi-step Conversions with Multi-task Learning in Targeted Display Advertising

**Source:** `/Users/fox/Projects/Awesome-Deep-Learning-Papers-for-Search-Recommendation-Advertising/04_Ranking/Multi-task/2021 (Meituan) (KDD) Modeling the Sequential Dependence among Audience Multi-step Conversions with Multi-task Learning in Targeted Display Advertising.pdf`
**Date analyzed:** 2026-08-16

## 1. Summary

Xi, Chen, Yan, Zhang, Zhu, Zhuang, and Chen (Meituan / Zhejiang University / CAS / Beihang University) target customer-acquisition advertising for **Meituan Co-Branded Credit Cards**, a five-step audience conversion path — *impression → click → application → approval → activation* — that is longer than the typical two- or three-step e-commerce funnel, has strictly ordered sequential dependence (y1 ≥ y2 ≥ ... ≥ yT), and has progressively sparser, more class-imbalanced positive labels at each later step (with *activation* additionally delayed, observed only within a T+14-day window after approval). The paper's central claim is that prior multi-task approaches cannot model this long-path sequential dependence well: the **Expert-Bottom pattern** (e.g., MMoE) only shares shallow bottom-layer representations across tasks and is not designed for ordered dependence; the **Probability-Transfer pattern** (e.g., ESMM, ESM²) transfers only a scalar probability product between adjacent tasks, discarding the richer vector-space representations near the output layer. The proposed **Adaptive Information Transfer Multi-task (AITM)** framework instead transfers a full vector representation between adjacent conversion-stage towers via an **Adaptive Information Transfer (AIT) module** — a self-attention-like mechanism (three single-layer MLPs h1/h2/h3 acting as Value/Query/Key on the concatenation of the previous stage's transferred information p_{t-1} and the current stage's tower output q_t) that adaptively learns *what* and *how much* information to transfer from step t-1 to step t. A **Behavioral Expectation Calibrator** loss term penalizes any case where a later-stage prediction ŷ_t exceeds the earlier-stage prediction ŷ_{t-1} for the same audience (max(ŷ_t − ŷ_{t-1}, 0)), enforcing the monotonic ordering the business requires and improving accuracy jointly. The full objective combines standard cross-entropy loss across all T tasks with the calibrator term (weighted by α).

Deployed within Meituan's multi-business ranking system to select which banner (credit card offer vs. other business ads) to show, weighted by predicted end-to-end conversion probability. Evaluated on a proprietary industrial dataset (4 tasks: click, application, approval, activation; 20M train / 3M validation / 26M test, with activation negative-sampled to 1% positive rate except in the test set) and the public Ali-CCP dataset (2 tasks: click, purchase). AITM beats LightGBM, MLP, ESMM, OMoE, MMoE, and PLE on both datasets: on the industrial data, AITM reaches approval AUC 0.8534±0.0011 and activation AUC 0.8770±0.0005, gains of +0.0142 and +0.0234 over LightGBM (both statistically significant at p<0.01 by paired t-test), versus PLE's smaller +0.0126/+0.0195 gains as the best baseline. A genuine online A/B test over two-week cycles shows AITM increasing the impression→approval and impression→activation conversion rates by 25.00% and 42.11% respectively, compared to MLP (itself already +16.95%/+17.55% over LightGBM).

## 2. Experiment Critique

**Design.** Offline comparison of AITM against six baselines (LightGBM, MLP, ESMM, OMoE, MMoE, PLE) on both a proprietary 4-task industrial dataset and a public 2-task Ali-CCP dataset, with a dedicated ablation on the number of tasks (T=1 to 4), a hyperparameter study (embedding dimension, calibrator strength α, positive-sample proportion λ), and a t-SNE case study visualizing the AIT module's learned representations against original and naively-transferred alternatives.

**Statistical validity.** Offline AUC is reported as mean±std over five runs with different random seeds (Table 2), and statistical significance is explicitly tested via paired-sample t-tests against the best baseline, with results marked "*" (p<0.05) and "**" (p<0.01) — the most statistically rigorous offline protocol among the four Multi-task papers read in this batch. The online A/B test reports only point-percentage gains (25.00%, 42.11%) with no confidence interval or significance test, run over consecutive two-week cycles per model pair (required for the T+14 activation delay to fully resolve).

**Online experiments.** A real production A/B test in the Meituan app, deploying LightGBM, MLP, and AITM successively to "tens of millions of traffic" per model, each pair compared over two consecutive weeks to allow the delayed activation feedback (T+14) to be observed. This is a genuine, delay-aware online deployment — notably more careful about label delay than the online tests in the other three papers in this batch, none of which have a delayed terminal label.

**Reproducibility.** The industrial (Meituan credit-card) dataset is proprietary and not released. The public Ali-CCP (Alibaba Click and Conversion Prediction) dataset is a standard, publicly available benchmark. The authors state the source code is released at a public GitHub repository (github.com/xidongbo/AITM) — the only one of the four papers in this batch with a released code artifact, though this is stated in text and was not independently verified by fetching the repository.

**Overall.** The strongest experimental design in this batch: statistically tested offline gains, a genuine delay-aware online test, a released public dataset for one of the two evaluation settings, and released source code — though the online gains themselves still lack confidence intervals.

## 3. Industry Contribution

AITM directly targets a documented industrial deployment gap: Meituan's credit-card ranking system must jointly serve multiple partner banks with different business priorities (some prioritizing rapid approval volume, others prioritizing longer-run activation rate), handled via a "Selector" component that lets the multi-task framework's approval and activation heads be weighted differently per bank at serving time (Figure 3), while the *click* and *application* auxiliary tasks — which have far more positive samples — supply richer transferred information to alleviate the class imbalance of the sparser *approval*/*activation* targets. The system is deployed in the live Meituan app ranking pipeline, feeding a multi-business ranking score `score = weight × ŷ` that determines which business's banner (credit card vs. other) is shown to a given audience. Engineering cost is modest: the AIT module adds three single-layer MLPs per adjacent task pair on top of an otherwise standard shared-embedding, per-task-tower architecture; the paper frames the design explicitly as general and flexible, noting any advanced tower architecture (NFM, DeepFM, AFM, sequence models) can be substituted without changing the AIT mechanism. Framed in recommender-engineering terms: the paper does not report a serving-latency figure directly for AITM, but does report end-to-end real-time serving via TF Serving with system-wide TP999/TP9999 latency under 20ms/30ms; feature engineering is unchanged (user and context features shared via a common embedding layer); and the multi-business selector is a directly reusable pattern for any recommender needing to arbitrate among several downstream businesses competing for the same limited exposure slot.

## 4. Novelty vs. Prior Work

AITM's claimed novelty is the Adaptive Information Transfer module, which transfers a full learned vector representation (not merely a scalar probability, as in the Probability-Transfer pattern) between adjacent tasks in a chain with explicit sequential dependence, combined with a Behavioral Expectation Calibrator loss enforcing the business's monotonic-ordering constraint. Prior work discussed and directly compared as baselines: **Ma, Zhao, Huang, Wang, Hu, Zhu, Gai, "Entire space multi-task model" (ESMM), SIGIR 2018** — cited and used as the canonical Probability-Transfer-pattern baseline, explicitly critiqued: "the Probability-Transfer pattern can only transfer simple probability information via the scalar product... richer and more useful representations in the vector space are ignored." **Wen, Zhang, Wang, Lv, Bao, Lin, Yang, "Entire space multi-task modeling via post-click behavior decomposition" (ESM²), SIGIR 2020** — cited as extending ESMM's Probability-Transfer idea by decomposing more tasks, inheriting the same scalar-transfer limitation. **Ma, Zhao, Yi, Chen, Hong, Chi, "Modeling task relationships in multi-task learning with multi-gate mixture-of-experts" (MMoE), KDD 2018** — the canonical Expert-Bottom-pattern baseline, critiqued for only sharing shallow bottom-layer representations. **Tang, Liu, Zhao, Gao, "Progressive Layered Extraction" (PLE), RecSys 2020** — the strongest Expert-Bottom-pattern baseline in this paper's results, separating task-shared and task-specific experts explicitly. **Vaswani et al., "Attention is all you need," NeurIPS 2017** — cited as the conceptual source of the self-attention-like Value/Query/Key design used in the AIT module. **Li, Zhang, Huang, Bao, Wang, Chen, "Modeling Bi-Directional Spatio-Temporal Dependence and Users' Dynamic Preferences for Missing POI Check-In Identification," AAAI 2021** — same author group, cited as related sequential-dependence modeling work.

## Project Relevance

Speaks directly and heavily to **Q2** (the AIT module is a concrete, general mechanism for propagating information between adjacent stages of an ordered, multi-step conversion chain — the closest architectural precedent in this batch to the project's four-stage cascade) and to **Q4** (learned, adaptive fusion between adjacent-stage heads via attention, rather than fixed multiplicative fusion as in ESMM/ESM²/HM3 — a genuinely different point on the fusion taxonomy). Also speaks to **Q3**: it is the only paper in this batch with an explicit named delay window (T+14) on its terminal label, making it the most relevant of the four for label-and-horizon precedent, even though 14 days is still short of the project's 7-30 day retention horizon. Touches **Q6** with the most rigorous offline statistical protocol (paired t-tests, 5-seed variance) and a genuinely delay-aware online test design in this batch.

Does **not** meaningfully address **Q5** (pure prediction throughout, no incrementality or causal treatment), **Q7** (no reciprocity, congestion, or two-sided fairness treatment — the "Selector" arbitrates among competing *businesses*, not between two human sides of a match), or **Q8** (no staged-migration narrative from a prior production system to AITM; it is deployed as a wholesale successor to LightGBM/MLP, not documented as an intermediate migration step).
