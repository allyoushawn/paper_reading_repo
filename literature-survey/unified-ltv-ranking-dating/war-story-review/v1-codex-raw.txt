    65	## Act 4 — What the field actually did
    66	
    67	Here is the arc the corpus describes. Years were verified against the notebook.
    68	
    69	**2008–2014 — the censored label.** Elkan and Noto (KDD 2008) established that an unlabeled sample is not a negative sample. Chapelle at Criteo (KDD 2014) turned that into the delayed feedback model, and showed that naive same-day labeling **underpredicts conversions by 21%**. That paper is the most-cited baseline in the entire corpus. Every later method that trains on a pending label descends from it.
    70	
    71	**2017–2019 — make it an MDP.** The first answer to "optimize the long term" was reinforcement learning. DRN at Microsoft (WWW 2018) folded user return frequency into the reward. Pinterest's notification volume control (KDD 2018) maximized site-wide DAU under a volume budget. SlateQ (Google, IJCAI 2019) decomposed a slate's long-term value into item-level values. Top-K off-policy REINFORCE (Google, WSDM 2019) scaled policy gradients to a huge action space.
    72	
    73	**2018–2022 — the cascade, and its proven bias.** In parallel the multi-task cascade matured. ESMM (Alibaba, SIGIR 2018) modeled CTR and CVR over the whole impression space, and became the corpus's second most-cited baseline. MMoE (Google, 2018) became the standard shared backbone. AITM (Meituan, KDD 2021) handled deeper funnels. Then Alibaba's own Multi-IPW and Multi-DR work (WWW 2020) proved ESMM biased, and ESCM² (SIGIR 2022) fixed it with a counterfactual regularizer.
    74	
    75	**2019–2025 — stop hand-tuning the blend.** This strand sits closest to our problem. PE-LTR (Alibaba, RecSys 2019) and Amazon's stochastic label aggregation (WWW 2020) started learning the weights. BatchRL-MTF (Tencent, KDD 2022) treated fusion as a session-level MDP, solved it with conservative Q-learning, and reported +2.55% dwell time and +9.65% positive-interaction rate at hundreds of millions of users. Tencent then published UnifiedRL and EnhancedRL (2024) as successors. Kuaishou's xMTF (WWW 2025) **replaced BatchRL-MTF's fixed log-sum formula** with formula-free monotonic fusion cells. The migration is documented end to end: fixed formula, then learned weights, then no formula at all.
    76	
    77	**2021–2022 — change the label, not the algorithm.** Two works showed you can buy much of the long-horizon win without RL. Google's user-response models (2021) added supervised auxiliary heads to REINFORCE, and the notebook confirms this **explicitly builds on** Chen et al. (2019). PinnerFormer (Pinterest, KDD 2022) trained one user representation against a dense multi-day future-action window instead of next-item prediction. The notebook confirms PinnerFormer **explicitly modifies** the all-action objective of SASRec (Kang and McAuley, 2018). Both arrows point backward.
    78	
    79	**2023–2025 — retention becomes the reward.** Kuaishou dominates this stretch. RLUR (WWW 2023) framed retention as an infinite-horizon MDP and minimized return-to-app time. Its online test ran about 150 days and moved app opens +0.450%, DAU +0.2%, and D7 +0.063%. TSCAC (WWW 2023) added constraints so engagement gains could not eat sparse interactions. GFN4Retention (KDD 2024) pushed a sparse session-level reward back onto item-level feedback. AURO (2025) **builds on RLUR** and attacks non-stationarity with a contrastive state-abstraction network.
    80	
    81	**2019–2026 — do not wait for the horizon.** Athey, Chetty, Imbens and Kang introduced the surrogate index (2019). Microsoft Research added a dynamically adjusted version. Spotify's Impatient Bandits (KDD 2023) combined partial activity traces through a Bayesian filter, and explained 50% of the prior variance from 8 days of traces. ★ **Correction applied:** my draft had Impatient Bandits citing Maystre et al. 2024, which would point forward in time.     82	
    83	**2024–2026 — unify the architecture.** Generative rankers arrived: HSTU (Meta, ICML 2024), OneRec and OneRec-V2 (Kuaishou, 2025), MTGR (Meituan, 2025), and Netflix's GenRec and GenPage (2026).     91	## Act 5 — The beliefs that broke
    92	
    93	The chronology is the easy half. The valuable half is where the survey overturned something we believed going in.
    94	
    95	**The Instagram Explore myth.** We treated Meta's Instagram Explore "value model" as settled precedent for unified long-term ranking. It is cited that way constantly. Reading its stated objective shows a **fixed-weight linear fusion over short-term engagement probabilities**, with no horizon and no delay handling stated anywhere. That reframed the whole project. We are not adopting a proven industry pattern. We are moving a research-stage idea into production. Plan accordingly.
    96	
    97	**A debiased prediction is not an effect.** This is the deepest reversal, and all three runs reached it independently. Methods that look causal — IPS weighting, doubly-robust estimation, ESCM² — mostly **debias a prediction**. One workplace put the consequence in one line: conventional outcome losses rank high-propensity users, not persuadable users. Our own product constraint said the same thing in plainer words. Retention conditional on exposure is not the effect of exposure, because active users retain regardless.
    98	
    99	**Most retention-RL evidence is circular.** Much of the RL-for-retention literature evaluates on the KuaiSim simulator. In KuaiSim, retention is drawn as `Geometric(p_ret)`, and `p_ret` rises mechanically with the immediate reward the policy is optimizing. The simulator assumes the conclusion. The survey adopted a hard rule: label every result as online A/B or simulator, and never compare the two. RLUR's online test survives that filter. Much of the rest does not.
   100	
   101	**Surrogates are safe but lossy.** We expected a validated surrogate to let us read a slow metric quickly. Netflix's study over 200 A/B tests and 1,098 arms found about 95% agreement between a 14-day and a 63-day read. On launch decisions it found **79% precision and 65% recall**, with zero false launches among tests significantly negative at 63 days. The framing flipped from "safe and sufficient" to "safe and lossy." Plan for missed true positives.
   102	
   103	**And you cannot validate a surrogate the obvious way.** This correction is sharper than the one above, and only one workplace surfaced it. Netflix's follow-up work shows that correlating user-level engagement with the long-run outcome is **biased**, and that the fitted slope can carry the **wrong sign**. The rule that follows is concrete. Do not validate a surrogate by correlating user-level likes with D30. Use a causal estimator across experiments instead.
   104	
   105	**More engagement is not more retention, and one system proved it on itself.** Pinterest's notification work (KDD 2018) and Duolingo's bandit study (KDD 2020) both found that **fewer** interventions can raise long-term engagement. TSCAC is the sharpest case, because the reversal happened inside a single deployment: optimizing WatchTime (+0.379%) and Share (+3.376%) drove **Comment down 0.619%**. That is the closest published analogue to our success paradox, where a good match ends the user's tenure.
   106	
   107	**SlateQ does not transfer.** We assumed SlateQ solved item-level credit assignment. Both of its licensing assumptions fail here. Users engage several candidates per session, so *Single Choice* breaks. A match needs a delayed action by the other person, so *Reward Dependence on Selection* breaks. Worse, Kuaishou's ItemA2C tried decomposing the RL critic to item level and it **failed**. The critic had to stay list-level. One workplace answers our credit-assignment question in three words: **most do not**.
   108	
   109	**Generative rankers unify the cascade, not the objective.** OneRec-V2 rewards a same-session watch-time quantile, and its own limitations section treats 7-day return as an evaluation metric only. MTGR trains on CTR and CTCVR. Netflix's GenRec supplies the number that settles it: **+0.115% on short-term engagement, and +0.006% on the long-term core metric**. The gain falls by a factor of roughly nineteen as the horizon lengthens. The architectural revolution and the objective question are separate problems.
   110	
   111	**Ranking on relevance alone breaks a two-sided market.** The numbers are stark. Naive ranking sends the top 20% of listings roughly 100% of requests. Market-clearing raises expected matches from 219.56 to 332.91 on a 200-user benchmark. A welfare-maximizing ranker gets more raw matches than a fair one (111.37 against 90.39) and carries **fourteen times the envy** (434 against 31). Reciprocity and capacity are a mandatory second stage, not a fairness patch.

   177	## Where we are, and where we are going
   178	
   179	**The arc in one sentence.** We set out to find the industry pattern for a single ranker trained on retention and revenue, found that the most-cited precedent does not do what it is cited for, and came away with a staged path whose first real step is not a model at all — it is proving we can read a long-horizon outcome early, and knowing how much that read costs us.
   180	
   181	**The founding premise survived, but only in half.** We wanted one model whose training objective is retention and revenue, with the blend gone. The survey endorses the first half without reservation: unify the **serving score**, learn the fusion, and take the hand-tuned blend out. It does not endorse the second half yet. Putting **incrementality** inside the training objective of a reciprocal, long-horizon ranker has no documented precedent in 120-plus industry sources. That is research, and it needs randomized exposure before it is even identifiable. Two of three runs still put it at the end of the road. One takes it off the road.
   182	
   183	**The standing plan** is the staged path the three runs broadly share:
   184	
   185	| Stage | Change | Gate to proceed |
   186	|---|---|---|
   187	| 0 | Instrument the join from event to 30-day retention and revenue. Log propensities and both-side actions. Ship nothing. | Replay reproduces assignment. Leakage tests pass. |
   188	| 1 | **Validate a surrogate against past experiments, with a causal estimator.** | Precision and recall measured, not assumed. |
   189	| 2 | Add long-horizon heads in shadow. Serving unchanged. | Heads calibrated on held-out horizons. |
   190	| 3 | Replace the hand-tuned blend with learned fusion. Move uplift out of serving. | Fusion beats the blend on a long-horizon metric. No match-quality regression. |
   191	| 4 | Add reciprocal allocation and a receiver-capacity penalty. | Match lift without overload or fairness regression. |
   192	| 5 | Only under logged randomized exposure, move incrementality inside the objective. | Cluster-robust interval agrees with a powered market experiment. |
   193	
   194	**Do not skip Stage 1.** Every later stage depends on reading a long-horizon result faster than the horizon, and on knowing how lossy that read is.
   195	
   196	**Load-bearing caveats we carry forward:**
   197	
   198	- **Item-level credit for a reciprocal, delayed outcome is unsolved.** SlateQ's assumptions fail here, and Kuaishou's own attempt to decompose the critic failed. Anything we build on this point is new work, not adoption.
   199	- **Prediction is not incrementality.** Keep the distinction on every card, every model and every review.
   200	- **The delayed-feedback literature runs at hours to days.** Our horizons run to weeks. The framing transfers. The exponential-delay assumptions and the calibration timescales are not validated at our horizon by any paper in the corpus.
   201	- **The mixed revenue stream has no precedent.** No paper models subscriptions and a la carte purchases together. Our two-head proposal is the one recommendation in this survey with no evidence either way, and it is labeled as such.
   202	- **Congestion is an architecture gap, not a modeling gap.** A per-receiver exposure quota needs coordination across viewers. A per-request ranker cannot express it.
   203	- **The worst measurement failure mode is interference through the training data.** A treatment changes what the shared model learns, which contaminates the control group with no direct user-to-user interference at all.
   204	- **The dating industry publishes almost nothing.** Match Group, Bumble, Coffee Meets Bagel, Tantan and Soul returned nothing usable. Grindr states publicly that it runs no discovery ranker, which makes it a useful negative control — unifying the ranker is not universal practice even within dating. CyberAgent and the CoupLink field experiment are the real exception. Everything else transfers from LinkedIn Jobs, Airbnb and online recruitment, and every transfer is an assumption.
   205	- **Our top-ranked pattern rests on a paper nobody could read.** Six sources are confirmed to exist and are permanently unreachable. Two matter: Netflix's *Reward Innovation for Long-Term Member Satisfaction* (RecSys 2023), described as the keystone of the reward-weighting pattern, and Meta's surrogate-metrics work (KDD 2026).

## (c) Reasoning arc and turning points

**CRITICAL — The conclusion conflates three different propositions.**

> “The survey endorses the first half without reservation: unify the **serving score**, learn the fusion, and take the hand-tuned blend out. It does not endorse the second half yet. Putting **incrementality** inside the training objective of a reciprocal, long-horizon ranker has no documented precedent…”

The README’s founding target is one model trained directly on retention and revenue. Incrementality is a separate research question and product constraint. The story collapses:

1. Replacing fixed post-hoc fusion with learned fusion.
2. Training predictive long-horizon retention/revenue heads.
3. Training on the incremental causal effect of exposure.

The evidence supports a staged experiment with the first two and shows that the third lacks precedent and identification. It does not show that “direct retention/revenue training” and “incrementality inside the objective” are the same rejected half.

Concrete fix: rewrite the thesis around these three layers. Say learned unified serving is supported as a candidate; direct long-horizon outcome prediction is plausible but unvalidated for this dating setting; causal incremental ranking remains research requiring randomized exposure.
